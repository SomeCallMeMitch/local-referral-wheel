# 04 --- Business Model and Economics

## Recommended validation pricing

Do not start by asserting a permanent $500/month price.

Suggested test:
- Founding cohort: $200--$300/month
- 6--10 founding members
- explicit pilot period: 90 days or 6 months
- founding price protected for a defined period
- require participation and feedback

Once outcomes exist:
- Core membership candidate: $395--$595/month
- Premium tier could add one-to-one sessions or implementation

## Illustrative membership economics

| Members | $250/mo | $400/mo | $500/mo |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | $1,500 | $2,400 | $3,000 |
| 8 | $2,000 | $3,200 | $4,000 |
| 10 | $2,500 | $4,000 | $5,000 |
| 12 | $3,000 | $4,800 | $6,000 |

At 10 members × $500/month, annual recurring membership revenue is $60,000 before expenses.

## Revenue layers

Treat these as modules, not assumptions.

### 1. Membership dues
Cleanest recurring revenue and should support the core operation by itself.

### 2. Premium coaching/advisory
Optional one-to-one sessions or intensive planning.

### 3. Implementation services
Campaign creation, automation, landing pages, follow-up systems, review programs, CRM cleanup, etc. Important: do not let this turn every meeting into an agency sales pitch.

### 4. Specialist marketplace / vetted providers
Members may need bookkeeping, ads, CRM, web, HR, recruiting, legal, insurance, etc. Referral compensation must be transparent and legally permissible for the industry.

### 5. SaaS fee
Initially bundled into membership. Later it could be a standalone fee for external groups.

### 6. Chapter leader license
Only after the operating system is proven. A future leader could pay setup/training + per-member SaaS/license fees.

### 7. Sponsorships
Potential for banks, payroll providers, software vendors, etc., but sponsorship must not undermine trust.

### 8. Events/workshops
Paid nonmember events can become acquisition funnels.

## The more scalable future model

A chapter-leader economics example, purely hypothetical:
- leader charges 10 members × $450 = $4,500/month;
- platform charges leader $49--$99/member/month;
- leader retains the majority and handles facilitation;
- platform supplies software, playbooks, training, content, support, benchmarks and brand.

At 100 chapters × 10 members × $75/member, platform revenue would be $75,000/month before other fees. This is not a forecast; it illustrates why proving the chapter operating system could create a larger business than operating one chapter.

## Costs to expect

- meeting space / food;
- payment processing;
- CRM/email/text;
- software hosting/API/AI usage;
- insurance;
- legal agreements;
- bookkeeping;
- acquisition/outreach;
- facilitator time;
- speakers/experts;
- optional implementation labor.

The initial pilot should intentionally avoid expensive overhead.

## Metrics that matter

**Acquisition**
- prospects contacted;
- conversations;
- qualified prospects;
- invitations;
- joins;
- CAC;
- days to fill a category.

**Engagement**
- attendance;
- commitments completed;
- referrals sent;
- referrals received;
- introductions accepted;
- dashboard usage;
- experiments launched.

**Economic outcome**
- referral pipeline value;
- closed referred revenue;
- gross profit where members are willing to disclose;
- revenue from reactivation;
- pricing lift;
- conversion lift;
- average ticket lift;
- estimated annualized impact.

**Retention**
- monthly churn;
- cohort retention;
- NPS/qualitative satisfaction;
- "value received / dues paid" ratio;
- category-seat waiting list.

## Critical pricing principle

The membership should become easy to justify when the dashboard can show:

- **Dues paid YTD: $3,000**
- **Attributed/referral revenue: $18,500**
- **Estimated profit improvements: $11,200**
- **Active opportunities: $24,000**
- **Actions completed: 19**

This is a stronger retention argument than "you attended six good meetings."
