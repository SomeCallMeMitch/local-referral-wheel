# Founding category architectures
Recommendation: explore homeowner service architecture first, subject to warm access. These are candidate seats, not recruited businesses. No specific city or ticket estimates have been researched. Ticket categories are relative planning assumptions: small < $1k; medium $1k–$5k; large > $5k collected first job; recurring value separate. Actual ranges vary widely.

## Architecture 1: homeowner maintenance and improvement
Eight target seats; launch with six only if each has at least two plausible incoming and two outgoing category paths supported by recent examples. Some flows will be uneven. Exclude transaction finance/brokerage from the initial fee experiment. Licensed trades still require jurisdiction-specific review; "home service" does not mean fee-safe.

| Priority / seat | Refers to → / receives from ← | Typical trigger / frequency hypothesis | Value consideration | Scope conflict / fee concern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Essential: plumbing specialist | → painter, flooring; ← cleaner, HVAC, homeowner-service contacts | Repair/disruption; regular service visits | Small–large; emergency vs planned jobs differ | Multi-trade firms overlap; licensing and fee review |
| Essential: HVAC specialist | → electrician; ← cleaner, electrician, plumber | Replacement, comfort, move-in; seasonal | Small maintenance to large replacement | Electrical bundle boundaries; licensing and fee review |
| Essential: electrician | → HVAC, painter; ← HVAC, landscaping, new-owner service | Upgrades/repairs; regular/episodic | Small–large | Scope licensing; combined trade providers |
| Essential: painter | → flooring, junk removal; ← plumber, flooring, electrician | Refresh/repair; episodic | Medium–large | General remodeling/cabinet/exterior boundaries |
| Strong addition: flooring | → painter, junk removal; ← painter, plumber, cleaner | Refresh or post-repair; episodic | Medium–large | Restoration/GC overlap; contract-fee rules |
| Strong addition: recurring cleaning | → trades, flooring; ← painter, flooring, junk removal | Repeated household contact | Smaller recurring jobs; source value may exceed recipient value | Don't price solely by larger peers' job values; check exact scope |
| Strong addition: landscape maintenance | → electrician, junk removal; ← cleaner, other home-service providers | Seasonal upkeep/outdoor work | Recurring or medium projects | Maintenance vs construction/irrigation licensed scope |
| Strong addition: junk removal | → painter, flooring, cleaner; ← those same categories | Clear-out, renovation prep | Small–medium; disposal costs affect margin | Moving, hauling and waste requirements; exact scope check |
| Situational: general remodeler | → multiple trades; ← painter/plumber | Large projects; slower | Large ticket, possibly thin pass-through margin | May consume many protected scopes; do not add casually |
| Situational: property manager | → many services; ← limited inbound paths | Turnovers/maintenance; high source potential | Anchor/customer role may fit better than paid peer | Agency/fiduciary conflicts; ownership and compensation disclosure |
| Avoid initially: mortgage/title/transaction brokerage | Many event links | Transactions | Property value is not provider revenue | Settlement/brokerage rules; no presumed fee permission |
| Avoid initially: healthcare/legal/insurance/financial advice | Sensitive family events | Episodic | High value doesn't cure restrictions | Separate legal/ethical/licensing review |

Founding eight = first eight rows. "Strong addition" is recruitment order, not a promise to wait until a later chapter. Recruit at least two high-contact sources early; avoid filling all seats with providers who only see customers once every decade.

## Architecture 2: local B2B operations
A viable alternate if founder access is stronger here. Target clients with 5–50 employees undergoing change. All ticket/frequency statements remain assumptions.

|---|---|---|---|

## Selection logic
Architecture 1 offers visible household events and potentially shorter outcome cycles. Architecture 2 may support higher recurring value, but has slower sales, overlap and stronger existing vendor ties. Either fails if members merely sell to one another: customers outside the group must create repeat demand.

Measure observed paths, not all theoretical pairings. At eight members there are 56 possible directed member pairs; most will not be useful. No target density percentage until actual events exist. Customer choice, capacity and quality always override seat protection. No requirement to route every customer through the network.
