# Original referral operating system

> Latest direction (2026-09-13): Vallejo, California and surrounding cities; founder role is connector; every fee/amount remains illustrative, with participating-business profitability first. See [current connector and fee-design update](../strategy/09_VALLEJO_CONNECTOR_AND_FEE_DESIGN.md). This update overrides conflicting earlier defaults.
Exploratory design for A; fee-specific additions apply only to E after legal/sector review. Independently designed from basic consent, matching, follow-up and evidence principles. Not an adopted competitor framework or legal agreement.

## Referral profile
Each provider supplies: business/category; exact protected service scope and exclusions; ideal customer; bad-fit customer; service area; high-value services; optional typical collected job value/range; top customer problems; 3–5 recent trigger events; listen-for phrases; two fit questions per trigger; preferred introduction method; urgency indicators; licensing/insurance checks where applicable; current capacity; named response owner; one-business-day acknowledgment commitment.
Review before acceptance and every 30 days. Example: "Interior repainting in the agreed service area; excludes cabinets, exterior work and general remodeling" is more useful than "painting." Never imply the group guarantees workmanship.

## Lifecycle
| Stage | Required action / responsible person | Record and exit condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Trigger recognized | Sender identifies a real need; checks urgency and fit | Anonymous note until prospect permits sharing |
| 2 Referral created | Sender records minimal need, intended recipient and source | Unique ID; pending-consent status; no mass circulation |
| 3 Prospect qualified | Sender asks basic fit/timing; recipient handles technical assessment | Need, area and timing fit; permission for named recipient |
| 4 Warm introduction | Sender connects prospect and provider in agreed channel | Consent timestamp, intro date, limited context; fee disclosure if relevant |
| 5 Acknowledged | Provider accepts, declines or requests clarification within 1 business day | Named owner and timestamp; refusal is acceptable |
| 6 Followed up | Provider contacts prospect within 2 business days or agreed urgency | Contact attempt/next action; prospect's choice governs |
| 7 Status updates | Provider updates after quote, booking or material change, at least weekly while active | Next-action date; no sensitive details to group |
| 8 Won/lost | Provider marks accepted job, declined/lost, withdrawn or deferred | Reason and date; "won" separate from invoice paid |
| 9 Value recorded | Provider records optional collected revenue/contribution for A; fee basis evidence required for E | Cash dates, refunds, evidence tier, unique job ID |
| 10 Feedback | Sender gets concise outcome with permission; coordinator asks about service quality | Close loop, complaint flag, lessons without unnecessary personal data |

Emergency needs go to appropriate emergency services or available qualified providers. The network is not an emergency dispatch service. Customer choice overrides the protected seat.

## Quality score: useful routing signal, not a points game
Consent is a mandatory gate, not a scored bonus. Score need specificity 0–2; service/geography fit 0–2; timing clarity 0–2; contact preference/availability 0–2. 6–8 = sufficiently qualified for pilot reporting; 3–5 = clarify; 0–2 = do not forward yet. The score does not predict a sale. Track actual recipient acceptance and complaints; do not pay for points or publish a leaderboard.

## Attribution and duplicates
Register sender, recipient, prospect/job ID and first introduction date before claiming value. Recipient flags an existing active opportunity within two business days. For pilot E, an opportunity with documented active contact/quote in the prior 90 days is excluded unless all parties specifically agree that a new distinct scope is attributable. An old customer alone neither proves nor disproves incremental work.

Default E1 introduction-desk fee window (E2 relationship coverage remains a separate choice in strategy/08): first qualifying job agreed within 90 days of the introduction; collected cash from that job tracked for up to 180 days from introduction. Revisit for long-cycle sectors before contracting. No lifetime commissions or automatic fee on all customer work. Multiple independent jobs require separate records.

Duplicate same prospect/scope/recipient: preserve earliest documented consented qualifying introduction; record later assistance without a second charge. Genuine joint sourcing needs agreement before introduction; the operator does not invent a split afterward. A chain creating jobs for several providers counts as multiple jobs but one originating event. Never multiply one invoice across referrals.

## Response and dispute handling
One-business-day acknowledgment; two-business-day initial contact; weekly active-status check. Missed response triggers one reminder; repeated failure pauses new routing. Do not spam the prospect to satisfy a metric.

Dispute steps: mark disputed and suspend fee invoicing on that portion; collect minimal timestamped evidence privately within five business days; facilitator hears both accounts; publish a written proposed resolution within five more business days. If unresolved, use agreed independent review/contract process. No public blame. Preserve the record and correct reports. Final legal terms remain to be drafted for the actual location.

## Reciprocity
Report received/sent accepted introductions, customer-care help, breadth of partner relationships and outcomes privately. Do not require equal counts or dollars. One well-placed introduction can be worth more than many weak ones.
- Gives much/receives little: check whether desired customers exist in network, map two possible inbound paths, ask what benefit they actually value, revise seat or offer free-source participation under E. Do not promise repayment in leads.
- Receives much/gives little: distinguish inability from unwillingness; coach trigger recognition; seek useful customer-care contributions. Persistent nonparticipation in A merits renewal review. E may legitimately have paid recipients with little outbound flow.
- No opportunities for anyone: category redesign, not tougher quotas.

## Gap identification
Monthly, map real events → needed services → accepted introductions → gaps. Prioritize missing categories with at least three observed unmet requests across at least two sources, not theoretically attractive average tickets. Track source concentration and members with no inbound/outbound opportunity. Add capacity alternatives when the protected member cannot respond; disclose the exception.

## Trust and removal
Before admission: scope verification, reference conversations, relevant public license checks and agreement to behavior terms. Pause routing immediately for credible serious safety, fraud or privacy complaints pending fair review. Remove for substantiated serious misconduct; repeated no-response after written notice and a 30-day improvement period; knowingly false referral/outcome records; persistent unsolicited selling; or repeated substantiated poor service. Record evidence and allow response; define refunds/termination before accepting dues.

## Privacy and no-spam
Permission must identify the intended recipient and purpose. Share minimum necessary contact/need; never distribute a customer list to the group. No automatic mailing-list enrollment, sale of contact data or unsolicited follow-on marketing. Each member controls their own customer relationship. Member reports are private; group view uses aggregates without sensitive revenue or customer identifiers. Delete unnecessary prospect contact details within 90 days after closure as a proposed policy, subject to required billing/legal retention and explicit consent. Keep redacted accounting evidence only as needed.

Fee terms, scope and consent are distinct: permission to introduce is not consent to marketing or agreement to compensation. See research/02 for limitations on fee models.
