Revenue bands screen out neither excellent small firms nor larger firms automatically. Capacity, service quality, response behavior and actual adjacent customer contact are stronger filters.

## Choose a customer, not merely a category
A founding candidate should be able to describe at least two recent situations in which a customer needed another proposed member's service. They should want more of a particular job type, have delivery capacity, and name someone responsible for prompt responses. A busy business that cannot take work may be a useful introducer but a poor paying recipient.

Prefer a deliberately adjacent mix within residential services over random local owners. Owner maturity still matters, but the wheel's first job is customer flow, not peer equivalence. If council-first wins, reverse that priority and select peers by decision complexity rather than referral adjacency.

## Job hierarchy for baseline A
**Primary:** Develop reliable, profitable new work through trusted introductions with less partner-management effort.

**Secondary:** Protect customer trust when recommending another provider; get introduced to suitable partners; improve speed and closure of existing referrals.

**Nice to have:** General peer advice, broad growth teaching, social connection, software, AI, benchmarks. These may become primary for B or C, which would represent a different product choice.

A member joins because they expect better-fit opportunities and trusted partners. A member stays because these relationships continue to help and require less chasing than managing them alone.

Join/stay divergence is dangerous if "referrals" sells a membership retained only by enjoyable meetings. It is useful only if the added retention benefit is explicit, valued, and economical. Ask renewers what they would separately pay for.

## Buyer and beneficiary distinctions
In E the paying customer is normally the receiving business; the introducer and referred prospect also need value and choice. The original arrangement's actual payer remains unconfirmed. In a customer-paid concierge, the prospect becomes the buyer and provider compensation can compromise the impartial promise. Do not blur these models.

## Disconfirming observations
- Owners need labor/capacity rather than demand: offer more leads would worsen their problem.
- Best sources will not recommend unfamiliar providers: recruitment is not enough; trust-building needed.
- Referrals happen without the operator: sell setup once or find a demonstrable ongoing job.
- Everyone wants to receive and nobody encounters triggers: no network supply.
- A general group wants advice far more than referrals: compare B; do not force A.
