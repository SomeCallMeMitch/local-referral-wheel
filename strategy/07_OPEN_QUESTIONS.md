# Open questions and next-session boundaries
## Founder questions asked this session
1. Who paid the original 10% and on what basis: first-job collected revenue, profit, or recurring revenue?
2. What geography and warm business-owner relationships are available?
3. Which work is enjoyable: partner/deal development, council facilitation, or implementation?

Founder answered that payment applied to any deal from the relationship and the ongoing job was keeping partners aligned and leads flowing both directions. This clarified the service role and broad coverage, but not the precise percentage basis/payer. Other questions remain open. No location inferred from device timezone.

## Questions that most change the model
- Was the old wheel operational, proposed, or partly tested? How many referrals, wins, invoices, payments and hours?
- Did the founder originate demand or only introduce firms to one another?
- Why would established partners continue paying after they know each other?
- Who encounters many triggers but has little reason to join a paid circle?
- Do providers want more work, better-margin work, steadier schedules, or trusted places to refer overflow?
- Is geographic proximity essential, or merely inherited from the chapter metaphor?
- Could one anchor buyer fund coordination for many providers?
- Would a finite paid partnership setup project serve the buyer better than membership?
- Is a refundable commitment payment valuable even when recurring dues are not?
- Which specific sectors/jurisdiction permit proposed compensation and operator activity?
- How much total cost, including fee + dues + participation time, can a member justify?
- What minimum founder hourly return and initial cash budget should be used?
- Would the founder prefer fewer higher-priced clients over more chapter members?
- What happens when demand exceeds a protected provider's capacity?
- Can repeated value arise without ongoing referrals, and would that be the same business?

## Exploration experiments before pilot commitment
Compare A, E and C using actual past situations, full cost examples and founder-role fit. B remains appropriate for owners primarily buying decision support. D should earn its complexity through evidence that the same buyers want both components.

Do not run five full pilots. Use interviews and one bounded paid micro-test to select one coherent 90-day offer. Concept ranking is provisional; a deposit is stronger evidence than a preference rating.

## Suggested Session 3
Begin with the summary and original-deal reconstruction. Choose one geography and list 12 reachable prospects spanning likely sources and recipients. Conduct or prepare interviews only as authorized; do not automatically contact anyone. Review which model is best supported and then revise one offer. If the founder still wants exploration, play through alternate event-specific, anchor-funded and customer-paid models before selling.

Not authorized by this pack: launch commitments, outreach messages, fee contracts, customer-data collection, software construction or final business selection.
