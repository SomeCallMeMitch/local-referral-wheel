## The opportunity, reconstructed
Owners do not need another repository of growth advice. A plausible job is getting suitable work from trusted relationships without personally maintaining every partnership or chasing every introduction. A separate job is completing neglected revenue improvements. A third is making difficult owner decisions with credible peers. These may be three businesses, not one offer.

The unusual opportunity is to organize around a **customer event** rather than a generic industry list: one real need creates a sequence of adjacent needs. The operator could make that sequence work reliably. A room full of noncompetitors does not automatically make a productive network.

Join because: "I want a dependable source of suitable jobs and trusted people to hand customers to." Stay because: "These relationships repeatedly produce useful opportunities and the handoffs actually get completed." Implementation can improve conversion, but must not become a substitute explanation when the referral promise fails.

## Recommended exploration order
1. Reconstruct the economics of the founder's original 10% model using actual examples.
2. Compare three front-runners: A, a lean dues-funded referral circle; E, a managed success-fee partnership service (including ongoing relationship stewardship); C, an implementation club.
3. Use B (advisory) and D (hybrid) as meaningful alternatives, not inevitable upgrades.
4. Run discovery before selling. The concrete pilot documents are a worked example, not launch authorization.

**Earlier dues-only comparison configuration, superseded as the leading founder-fit option by E2/E3:** A, a managed referral circle of eight established residential service owners in one overlapping service area, $199/month, no transaction fee, one 75-minute monthly meeting, manual follow-up. Six is the launch floor; ten the pilot cap. Start with eight complementary home-maintenance/improvement specialties, not a broad real-estate-finance chapter. Geography and category rules require local confirmation.

This is a benchmark for learning, not a dismissal of pay-per-deal. Model E becomes preferable if the founder can repeatedly maintain productive relationships or materially coordinate incremental opportunities, partners accept collectible fees, margins support them, and the specific activities and payment arrangements are permissible. A lower base payment plus a success fee is a serious E variant.

## Why this benchmark
It isolates willingness to pay for reliable relationships and handoffs; can stand on dues without imaginary licensing income; and has fewer collection disputes than E. It also has a serious weakness: $199 is expensive compared with free direct partnerships and may compare poorly with established referral organizations. The offer must earn its premium through service, not simply fewer meetings.

Do not start with the whole hybrid. A facilitator can hide poor referral performance behind workshops, making the pilot uninformative. Conversely, if owners pay enthusiastically for an implementation sprint but barely value partner matching, pursue C rather than relabeling it a wheel.

## Most consequential findings
- A 10% revenue fee consumes one-third of gross profit at a 30% gross margin, before other acquisition and operating costs. It may work for certain incremental, attractive deals; it is not a universal rate.
- Membership, refundable commitment deposits, and transaction fees solve different problems: operating funding, participation incentives, and outcome alignment.
- A brokered network need not require equal giving and receiving. A genuine two-sided model may have free introducers and paying recipients. That changes fairness, incentives and workload.
- Gross revenue is not incremental profit. Attribution, incrementality and cash collection need separate records.
- Exclusivity should protect a narrow in-group seat, not restrict customers' choice or outside partnerships.
- Recurring dues are not evidence of durable recurring value; repeating the same workshop can exhaust the reason to renew.
- The founder's preferred job matters. A successful deal desk can be an unpleasant collection business; a successful council can become a calendar full of meetings.

## Changes from original thesis
Keep trigger recognition, consented warm handoffs, quality control and lightweight follow-up. Question mandatory exclusivity, owner-only attendance, workshops, scorecards, dashboards and universal reciprocity. Defer custom SaaS, AI advice, curriculum, licensing, benchmarks, marketplace and broad KPI integrations. Remove guaranteed referrals, total-business-growth ROI claims and undefined blanket lifetime commissions from the working offer. Keep recurring relationship-derived fee coverage explicitly open for E2; see 08_RELATIONSHIP_FEE_EXPLORATION.md.

The first success criterion is not "we designed a complete system." It is choosing a small next experiment that can distinguish businesses worth building.
