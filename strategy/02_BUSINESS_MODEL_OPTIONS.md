# Business model options
All sizes, prices, hours and ratings are **assumptions for comparison**, not market quotes. Hours are steady-state monthly operator hours, excluding initial setup; acquisition is discussed separately. A is the comparison baseline, not a settled destination.

## Five distinct businesses

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Scoring and ranking
1–10; higher is better, including founder workload where 10 means lighter/more manageable. Weights prioritize validation, not eventual valuation. No score is customer evidence. Clarity 15%; appeal 15%; willingness to pay 15%; retention 10%; validation ease 15%; workload 15%; differentiation 5%; scale 5%; defensibility 5%.

A ranks first in this judgment exercise narrowly; C and E tie for second. E becomes 7.50 if known lead supply raises validation 7→9, workable fee collection raises workload 5→7, and WTP 6→7. C becomes 7.20 if a repeatable asset lowers workload burden 5→8. Thus the next evidence should concern supply, collection and founder delivery skill, not decimal scoring.

## Play-throughs: what each actually feels like
- **A:** A painter notices a customer's damaged flooring, obtains permission and introduces the flooring specialist. The operator checks acknowledgment and completion. If that would happen anyway, monthly dues need another concrete justification.
- **B:** An owner is considering a first manager. Peers help challenge assumptions and the owner chooses a hiring test. No referral is needed for the product to succeed.
- **C:** Four owners bring 20 stale estimates each; each implements an authorized follow-up sequence and reviews responses. An owner without usable records may need separate preparation, not unlimited facilitator rescue.
- **D:** The group passes referrals and completes a lab. A member who attends only labs may be subsidizing a network they do not value. Component usage and separate willingness to pay matter.
- **E:** The desk identifies a need, routes it to a qualified recipient and follows the agreed first job through payment. If the desk merely invoices friendships, partners bypass it. Continued origination, quality filtering and administration must earn the fee.

## More concepts worth keeping alive
| Concept | Distinct buyer/value logic | Cheap disconfirming test |
|---|---|---|
| Event-specific pod | Only "move-in readiness" or "commercial opening"; temporary event sequence | Map 10 recent events and find paid need for coordination |
| Free introducers, paid recipients | Reward customer care without forcing every source to buy membership | Secure 3 sources willing to introduce without cash incentives |
| Anchor-funded network | A property manager or service company funds vetted partner access | Ask anchor to fund a narrow service trial, with conflicts disclosed |
| Paid partner-setup sprint | One-time mapping/intros; no pretended recurring need | Sell a bounded setup project; see whether ongoing admin is wanted |
| Customer-paid concierge | Consumer/business buyer pays for coordination, provider fees absent | Identify one client willing to pay for impartial coordination |
| Seasonal collaboration campaign | Partners run a finite campaign instead of joining permanently | Execute one consent-based joint campaign and measure incremental work |
| Small commitment bond | Refundable payment tests attendance rather than generates revenue | Compare actual onboarding completion; never count deposit as revenue |
| Done-with-you relationship manager | One firm pays for systematic partner development outside a chapter | Sell a narrow retainer and record relationship-maintenance hours |

These are exploration branches, not eight more launch products. Reject the assumption that a chapter or subscription must exist. A one-time profitable service can be a better discovery than a weak recurring offer.

## Update after founder clarification
E has two versions: E1 coordinates discrete introductions; E2 manages ongoing relationships and receives fees on eligible relationship-generated deals. The table/hours above are E1 assumptions; E2 deserves a separate economic and buyer test. See [relationship-fee exploration](08_RELATIONSHIP_FEE_EXPLORATION.md). The founder's clarification raises E2's relevance; it does not establish fee collections or profitability. Keep it a front-runner alongside A and C, rather than treating first-job fees as a settled requirement.
