# Session 8 — Remington Solar contractor cross-promotion summary

Completed public-source evaluation: September 15, 2026.

## What this session added

This session evaluates a national B2B variation of the referral concept. Remington Solar publicly recruits roofers, solar installers, HVAC companies, insulation contractors, general contractors, retailers, and distributors to sell solar attic fans. Its site confirms a pitch-board sales tool with a miniature demonstration fan, wholesale dealer pricing, warranty support, homeowner-lead routing, and a separate 5% affiliate program. The Facebook advertisement's claim that the demonstration kit is free was observed by the founder but was not independently confirmed on Remington's public pages.

The cleaner model is a **permissioned contractor cross-promotion network**, not a list exchange. Each vendor retains its own contacts, sends approved creative through its own system, and routes interested contractors to a co-branded page where they affirmatively request information. Only self-submitted request data would pass to the named recipient.

## Provisional candidate ranking

The strongest public partnership evidence was found for:

1. Roofr — explicit manufacturing-partner path aligned with increasing contractor demand and product sales.
2. JobNimbus — relevant contractor audience and multiple strategic, marketing, technology, and referral partnership tracks.
3. RoofTalk — public route for vendors serving the roofing industry.
4. RainDrop Gutter Guard — complementary roofline product and visible industry-partner relationships.
5. BirdBlocker — direct solar-professional audience and public partner route.

RoofersCoffeeShop is the strongest paid validation-channel hypothesis. Gutterglove Pro, CompanyCam, VELUX, Gemstone Lights, Bird Barrier, and Roof Maxx are additional candidates. Solatube is excluded because it sells solar attic fans and ventilation products directly.

These rankings are analytical judgments only. Public partner pages show a mechanism or partnership culture; they do not prove willingness to run this campaign, provide access to an audience, or promote Remington.

## Revenue-model conclusion

The conceptually cleanest operator revenues are:

- a fixed campaign-management fee;
- a fixed sponsorship or placement fee; or
- reciprocal in-kind promotion.

A fixed B2B dealer-activation payment or direct-product affiliate percentage may be possible with appropriate legal and contract review. The initial model should exclude compensation tied to a homeowner appointment, home-improvement contract, project price, payment, or contractor selection.

This is not a legal conclusion. CAN-SPAM, call/text consent, state privacy law, affiliate disclosure, contractor licensing, and state referral-compensation rules remain applicable to the actual facts.

## Material diligence gates

Before this option could be considered ready for real-world validation, Remington would need to document:

- actual contractor-list size, engagement, and trade/state distribution;
- demo-kit terms and dealer-funnel conversion data;
- first-order, installation, and reorder performance;
- wholesale economics, installation requirements, warranty, and callback history;
- whether it would reciprocally promote an approved noncompeting partner; and
- substantiation for energy, temperature, moisture, and HVAC-savings claims.

The product-claim gate is material. DOE Building America and ENERGY STAR caution that powered attic fans can pull conditioned air, increase energy use, or create pressure and combustion-safety problems when the ceiling is poorly sealed or intake ventilation is inadequate. This does not prove Remington's products are ineffective in every suitable installation, but it makes universal savings claims inappropriate without product- and installation-specific support.
