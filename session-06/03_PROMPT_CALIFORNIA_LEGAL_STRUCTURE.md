## Exact legal questions

Research current California law and authoritative guidance governing these arrangements:

### 1. Pure referral or finder’s-fee arrangement

- May an unlicensed network operator receive a fee, percentage or other consideration from a licensed home-improvement contractor for introducing a homeowner?
- Does the result change if payment is for a qualified lead, appointment, signed contract, completed work or collected customer payment?
- Does it matter whether the homeowner already intended to hire someone, whether the operator recommends one contractor or several, or whether the operator makes no sales representations?
- How do California Business and Professions Code §7157(a), (c) and (d), related sections and CSLB interpretations apply?
- Identify any statutory exceptions and explain their practical boundaries. Do not infer an exception merely because compensation is called marketing, consulting or administration.

### 2. Home Improvement Salesperson structure

- What activities make someone a Home Improvement Salesperson under §§7152–7155.5?
- Can one registered HIS currently represent multiple contractors, and what employment notification must each contractor file?
- Must the HIS be a common-law/W-2 employee, or can any independent-contractor relationship qualify under contractor law and California worker-classification law? Analyze both CSLB terminology and Labor Code/DIR/EDD rules.
- From whom may the salesperson accept compensation for a particular transaction?
- May the salesperson generate opportunities through relationships with other businesses, and what disclosure or identification must occur with the homeowner?
- Can the operator merely introduce and hand off, or would the lawful role require solicitation, sales, negotiation or other continuing duties?
- Could the same person be associated with a painter, flooring contractor and window-covering contractor at once?
- What registrations, fingerprints, renewals, notices, records and contractor responsibilities apply?
- What liabilities or disciplinary exposure would the operator and contractors assume?

### 3. Bona fide marketing or partner-management service

- Can a separate company provide advertising, marketing, appointment setting, lead qualification, CRM administration or partner-management services to contractors?
- Which payment methods are potentially lawful: fixed project fee, hourly fee, cost-per-lead, cost-per-qualified-appointment, percentage of a contract, success fee or revenue share?
- At what point would compensation become a prohibited referral fee, an inducement or unlicensed sales activity?
- Does payment for substantial documented services change the §7157 analysis, and what evidence distinguishes real services from relabeling a commission?
- Analyze whether the founder’s rejection of monthly membership fees still leaves any finite or usage-based lawful structure consistent with his preferences.

### 4. Contractor-to-contractor and other licensed-party arrangements

- Explain §7157(d)’s language concerning payments to another contractor, salesperson or licensee.
- Can licensed contractors compensate one another for actual work, marketing, referrals or sales? Under what circumstances?
- Would the founder need a contractor license, an HIS registration, employment by each recipient, or another credential?
- Can a business entity receive the commission if the individual performing regulated work is registered or licensed?

### 5. Non-contractor categories and mixed networks

- Which likely Benicia categories avoid the contractor-specific restriction: professional organizing, ordinary cleaning, estate-sale services, interior decorating without installation, photography, event services or similar work?
- Identify other California or federal referral restrictions that could apply to real-estate brokers, mortgage/settlement services, insurance, legal, medical, moving or other regulated categories. Use these to mark categories green, conditional or unsuitable; do not expand into an unfocused survey of every profession.
- In a mixed network, can unregulated source businesses refer work to a licensed contractor while only the network operator is compensated? Analyze the actual payment path rather than assuming the source’s lack of a license solves the problem.

### 6. Consumer, contract, privacy and payment issues

- What consumer disclosures, consent or conflict disclosures may be required or prudent when a supposedly trusted recommendation is compensated?
- What records would substantiate the basis and timing of compensation?
- How should cancellations, refunds, chargebacks, change orders, nonpayment and commission clawbacks be treated conceptually?
- Identify material California privacy, telemarketing or lead-generation rules only if they actually apply to permissioned introductions and a founder-operated CRM.
- Identify tax reporting or business-license issues that materially affect the operating structure, without turning this into general small-business boilerplate.

### 7. Transferability and future sale

- Which potentially lawful structures are transferable to a buyer of the operating network?
- Which depend on the founder’s personal HIS registration, employment relationships or trust and therefore reduce saleability?
- If the founder later licenses an original operating system to other connectors, identify franchise, business-opportunity or earnings-claim issues worth researching. Keep this secondary to solving the California operating model now.

## Required sources and research standard

Use current primary and official sources wherever possible:

- California Legislative Information for the current Business and Professions Code and Labor Code
- CSLB statutes, regulations, license-law reference book, HIS pages, forms and published enforcement guidance
- California DIR/DLSE and EDD for worker classification
- California Department of Real Estate where real-estate compensation is discussed
- CFPB, HUD or federal statutes/regulations for RESPA issues
- California Attorney General/CPPA for directly relevant consumer or privacy rules
- reported California cases, Attorney General opinions or administrative decisions if they materially interpret the disputed language

Secondary law-firm or trade commentary may help identify issues but cannot establish the conclusion by itself. State the date checked for every time-sensitive rule. Quote statutory language sparingly and link directly to the controlling section. Separate:

- binding statute or regulation;
- regulator guidance or enforcement position;
- court or administrative interpretation;
- secondary commentary;
- your inference;
- unresolved question.

Do not rely on a 2010 CSLB newsletter alone when newer statutes, current pages or decisions are available. Check whether cited provisions have changed through the current research date.

## Structures to test, not assume

Build and evaluate at least these candidate structures:

1. Unlicensed finder paid only after a completed/collected home-improvement job.
2. Registered HIS employed by one contractor and paid sales commission.
3. Registered HIS associated with multiple contractors and paid only by the represented contractor for its transaction.
4. Independent marketing/partner-management company paid per lead or appointment.
5. Independent marketing/partner-management company paid a fixed fee for documented services unrelated to closed-job value.
6. Network funded by a non-contractor anchor or source business.
7. Commission network limited to categories outside contractor regulation.
8. Hybrid in which contractor referrals are unpaid but commissions come from legally compatible categories.

For each, give a **green / conditional / red** finding, supporting authority, remaining uncertainty, required registrations/relationships, founder duties, expected administrative burden, fit with brief weekly conversations, commission-income potential and transferability.

7. **Transferability analysis:** identify what a purchaser could acquire and what personal registrations/employment relationships would have to be recreated.

9. **Recommendation for contractor research:** state which contractor categories and relationship structures the parallel contender-research chat should prioritize or exclude once this answer is available.
10. **Correction notice:** identify any claims in Sessions 3–6 that should be revised because of the legal findings.

Do not stop at “consult a lawyer.” If more than one interpretation is plausible, show both, explain the consequence of each and identify the most conservative workable design.
