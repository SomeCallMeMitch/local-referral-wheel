# 07 --- Chapter Operating System

## Ideal early member

Target established owners rather than startups.

Candidate characteristics:
- meaningful existing customer base;
- enough revenue that $250--$500/month is reasonable;
- local reputation worth protecting;
- responsive;
- can both give and receive referrals;
- willing to share non-sensitive business metrics;
- interested in improving the business;
- not chronically promotional;
- decision-maker attends personally.

## Category exclusivity

A seat should be exclusive only where overlap would inhibit trust/referrals.

Create:
- category;
- subcategory;
- protected scope;
- geography;
- exceptions;
- conflict review process.

Do not promise overly broad exclusivity that prevents filling the group.

## Recruitment proposition

Early version:

> We are assembling a private founding group of established, noncompeting local business owners. The group is designed to generate strategic referrals and work on practical revenue improvements, with results tracked so members can see whether the membership pays for itself.

## Recruitment method

Founder-led outreach is preferable for validation:
- warm network;
- LinkedIn;
- chamber directories;
- Google Maps/business lists;
- trade associations;
- local professional groups;
- referrals from recruited members.

Avoid spending heavily on ads until conversion language is known.

## Onboarding

Every member completes:
1. business profile;
2. ICP;
3. best customers/jobs;
4. exclusions;
5. service area;
6. top 5 trigger events;
7. ideal introduction;
8. current growth goals;
9. baseline KPIs;
10. current obstacles;
11. confidentiality and conduct agreement.

## Suggested cadence

Test two shorter sessions per month rather than immediately adopting a full-day board model.

### Meeting A --- Growth Lab

- KPI/win check;
- teaching/framework;
- implementation exercise;
- each member chooses action/experiment;
- commitments entered.

### Meeting B --- Council + Referral

- referral/opportunity review;
- member issue(s);
- peer questions/advice;
- decisions;
- accountability;
- relationship/trigger spotlight.

Alternative: one 2.5--4 hour monthly meeting. Test attendance and preference.

## Meeting rules

- confidential;
- no direct competitors;
- no unsolicited pitching;
- advice follows questions;
- be specific;
- disclose conflicts/commissions;
- referral recipient responds quickly;
- member owns final decisions;
- commitments are recorded;
- value is measured without exaggeration.

## Referral SLA

Example:
- acknowledge referral within one business day;
- contact referred party within agreed period;
- update sender at defined stages;
- never spam or add contact to marketing without permission;
- record outcome;
- protect the referring member's reputation.

## Member health review

Monthly facilitator dashboard:
- attendance;
- commitments completed;
- referrals given/received;
- unresolved referrals;
- economic value;
- participation;
- satisfaction;
- relationship imbalances.

## Retention conversation

Do not wait until cancellation. Quarterly:
- what value has been created?
- what hasn't worked?
- what is the member's next 90-day target?
- which network relationships are underused?
- what experiment should be run?
- does category fit remain correct?

## Governance risks

- weak member quality;
- overpromising referrals;
- category disputes;
- members selling aggressively to one another;
- low participation;
- one member taking more than giving;
- confidentiality breach;
- bad referral harming another member's reputation;
- facilitator becoming the bottleneck;
- inflated attribution;
- regulated referral-fee issues.

These require written policies before scale.
