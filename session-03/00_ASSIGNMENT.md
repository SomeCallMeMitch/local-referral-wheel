## NEW FOUNDER INPUT

The original Referral Wheel was not merely theoretical.

The founder previously worked with two complementary e-commerce service companies whose founders had similar customers but did not compete.

The arrangement worked like this:

- The companies met regularly.
- Each company looked for suitable opportunities among its stronger clients.
- When one company identified a customer who might need the other company’s service, it made a referral.
- The receiving company paid the founder when the referral became a deal.
- Payment worked in both directions.
- The founder’s compensation was roughly 10% of the deal or, in some cases, approximately one month of revenue on an annual recurring-service contract.
- The arrangement produced several actual deals for both sides.
- Typical founder compensation could be several thousand dollars on a successful deal.
- The model worked for a period of time.

A major failure point appeared when the founders stopped personally participating and delegated the relationship to subordinates. Referral flow weakened substantially.

The founder later attempted another referral relationship with one of the same companies and a different partner. Both parties initially showed interest, but the arrangement did not ultimately launch because of issues outside the founder’s control.

### Founder’s view of future compensation

The company receiving the revenue should generally pay the fee.

For a one-time project, such as roofing, the fee may be a one-time payment.

For a recurring-service business, such as pest control, compensation could instead be:

- an upfront commission;
- recurring monthly compensation;
- a percentage over a defined period;
- or another negotiated structure.

The founder believes that where he creates and actively manages a valuable partner relationship, future business generated through that relationship should remain compensable even if a participant later leaves the formal group but continues benefiting from the relationship.

He is open to:

- a long defined term such as five or ten years;
- declining fee percentages over time;
- lower fees after active group participation ends;
- other structures that fairly compensate the value created without becoming unreasonable.

Do NOT assume perpetual fees are legally or commercially viable. Analyze them critically.

### Founder’s intended ongoing role

The founder does not view the product as simply making introductions.

The intended role includes:

- selecting complementary businesses;
- bringing them into the network;
- maintaining partner relationships;
- encouraging and reminding partners to identify referral opportunities;
- tracking referrals;
- checking on referrals that were received;
- following up on whether leads were contacted;
- tracking whether deals closed;
- resolving dropped handoffs;
- helping keep value flowing in both directions;
- replacing weak or inactive partners;
- identifying new partner combinations;
- expanding into additional local wheels or territories;
- using CRM/SaaS/AI to make this increasingly systematic.

The founder also sees possible ancillary workflow tools such as automated or handwritten pre-contact notes to warm up a referred prospect before the receiving business calls.

Treat these as ideas to analyze, not mandatory features.

### Founder’s preferred scale

The founder does NOT currently envision a giant 40–50-company group.

He believes smaller, tightly connected wheels probably make more sense.

Potential future scale could come from:

- multiple small wheels;
- multiple cities/territories;
- software;
- AI;
- standardized operating systems;
- additional coordinators/operators.

### Important founder instruction

Do NOT simply evaluate the old Referral Wheel and decide whether to reproduce it.

The goal is broader:

**Use the original working model as evidence, then invent the strongest possible business models around the underlying opportunity.**

It is acceptable, and desirable, to finish with several strong business concepts rather than forcing one premature answer.

If the original concept has a fatal flaw, say so.

If there is a better business hidden nearby, find it.
