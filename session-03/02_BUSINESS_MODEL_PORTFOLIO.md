# 02 — Business model portfolio
2026-09-13. Session 03 letters differ from Session 02. All prices, group sizes and hours are modeling hypotheses; none are validated or fixed by founder history. Detailed pricing/economics are separate deliverables.

## Separate product from payment and scale
A/B/C share a managed-wheel product with different risk allocation; calling them three inventions would be misleading. F is an expansion configuration and H a later productization path. To ensure genuinely distinct businesses, also compare I (handoff operations) and J (finite partnership activation). The six distinct buyer/jobs are managed mutual network, one buyer's portfolio, customer-event coordination, anchor ecosystem, existing-network operations, and finite activation. Software sales are a seventh only after separate operator demand.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| B Managed hybrid wheel | Receiving participants fund availability and success; sources may be free | Small base + capped success fee or monthly credit | Same stewardship plus reconciliation; eligible receipts reported privately | 3–5 firms, operational check-ins rather than broad networking |

## Provisional ranking for testing
1. D: single-buyer partner management — strongest buyer clarity and connector fit.
2. I: handoff reliability — potentially easier proof and durable operating job.
3. B: small managed wheel with modest creditable base — closest mutual-network test; must earn complexity.
4. G: anchor-funded provider coordination — compelling if an anchor values coverage.
5. C: success-only — use when real supply and permissible collectible fees are already plausible.
6. J: activation sprint — strong discovery/entry product, not a membership fiction.
7. A: dues-only wheel — useful comparator; weaker link to founder's preferred outcome economics.
8. E: event service — useful but scope creep risk.
9. F: territories — only after one unit proves itself.
10. H: standalone software — later customer discovery, not current business thesis.

These rankings are judgment, not a market score. Top five stress-tested in 04 are D, I, B, G and C. J remains a possible entry method for D rather than a sixth full pilot.

## What happens if this works?
D/I may become a small outsourced partner-operations business with coordinators, not a franchise. B/C may split into role-specific sources/recipients instead of equal memberships. G may evolve into anchor operations with provider backups. No model requires a 40–50-company group. A mature pair graduating from active management can be a successful finite engagement rather than a retention failure.
