# 03 — Hybrid pricing, repeat revenue and exit compensation
2026-09-13. All dollar amounts/rates below are **invented arithmetic examples**. Historical 10% is not the optimization target. Sector-specific permissibility must be established independently; see 08 when complete.

## First principle
Fee budget = collectible job revenue − delivery/selling/financing costs − appropriate risk allowance − recipient's required retained contribution − allocated base fees. Required retained contribution must cover overhead, capacity opportunity cost and desired profit. An affordable fee must also cover operator effort and failed opportunities. If these bounds do not overlap, the job is unsuitable for this service at that workload.

Recurring contract value is not cash in hand. One month of revenue can consume most of several months' contribution in a low-margin service. Pest-control compensation must consider route density, service cost, cancellations, seasonality and actual customer tenure; no tenure assumed.

## Sixteen payment structures
Rank is judgment for a small connector operation, not a price recommendation. "Upside risk" identifies who gives up value in strong periods. S/L/R = small jobs / large projects / recurring contracts.

| Rank / structure | Logic / dry-period risk | Upside exposure | Admin / bypass / perceived fairness | S / L / R suitability and trust issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Recipient retainer, bounded workload | Pays for active partner operations; recipient funds dry months | Operator may underprice busy periods | Low billing; lower bypass because direct contact allowed | S: pooled volume; L: good if active portfolio; R: good. Must show real work |
| 2 Monthly minimum credited against earned fees | Funds availability without simply adding charges; recipient minimum/operator excess effort | Operator sacrifices some upside vs additive | Medium; transparent reconciliation | S: needs aggregate value; L/R: viable if bounded. Credit expiry must be clear |
| 3 Margin-informed fixed/capped win fee | Agree affordable outcome price upfront; operator funds dry months | Operator gives up large-job upside | Medium; moderate reporting/bypass | S: only low admin; L: predictable; R: first-term scope. No arbitrary fee ceiling based only on ticket |
| 4 Low base + capped success fee | Funds service and outcomes; shared dry risk | Recipient pays both in good months | Medium-high; can feel double charged | All conditional on total-cost ceiling; define which service base buys |
| 5 Setup + modest maintenance | Finite activation paid, ongoing service distinct | Operator loses uncaptured downstream upside | Low-medium; low rationale for bypass | Strong for partnership formation; recurring work must exist |
| 6 Fixed fee per collected closed deal | Easy to understand; operator carries dry risk | Operator foregoes large-job upside | Lower than percentage; still prove paid win | S: expensive if thin margin; L: good; R: first event only |
| 7 Marginal fee bands | Effective rate declines as value rises | Operator limits large-job share | Medium; explain bands and exclusions | L especially; S/R only after cost checks. Avoid whole-job bracket cliffs |
| 8 Active-service-only fee | Fees tied to defined eligible work while stewarding | Operator may lose later relationship value | Medium; less exit resentment | Good where continuing contribution is visible; termination timing can invite gaming |
| 9 Anchor base + recipient fee | Anchor buys coverage; recipient buys wins | Risk of two parties paying for same work | High conflict/disclosure burden | Works only if services and interests distinct; no paid priority that harms customer |
| 10 Defined recurring revenue share | Small share of actual collected receipts for a specified term | Operator bears cancellations; recipient shares long-duration revenue | High monthly reconciliation; moderate bypass | R only if low-admin pooled reporting; stop on cancellation/refund |
| 11 Declining repeat commissions | Lower compensation for later jobs/renewals | Both compromise on continuing value | High cohort/date tracking | R/large repeats possible; weak without an attributable customer definition |
| 12 Annual management fee + lower deal fee | Funds year of availability | Recipient risks prepaying unused service | Medium; cancellation/refund disputes | Established evidence only; annual cash is not earned immediately |
| 13 Dues waived after threshold | Reward successful volume; operator dry risk remains partly covered | Revenue can fall at threshold | Medium; avoid retroactive cliff | Easier to express as creditable minimum; customers may time reporting |
| 14 30/60/90 days free then dues | Removes early payment risk; operator bankrolls launch | Later conversion uncertain | Billing transition/friction | Useful only bounded learning budget; free signups not WTP evidence |
| 15 Success-only founding period | Test referral engine before dues | Operator funds all initial work | High collection dependence | Known pipeline only; don't promise same rate forever |
| 16 Long post-exit relationship royalty | Compensates creation after participation ends | Recipient bears long-lived cost; operator bears enforcement risk | Highest attribution/audit/bypass/friction | Possible narrow negotiated use; poor general default |

The rankings assume a connector who needs sustainable work. A recipient with strong existing pipeline could prefer rank 3 over a retainer. A payer refusing any base is not automatically a bad prospect, but its dry-period costs need a credible source.

## Do the hybrid arithmetic correctly
Invented example per payer: base B=$80 and eligible monthly success fee F.
| F | Additive B+F | Creditable minimum max(B,F) | Threshold waiver: B+F until F>=400, then F |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $0 | $80 | $80 | $80 |
| $200 | $280 | $200 | $280 |
| $399 | $479 | $399 | $479 |
| $400 | $480 | $400 | $400 |
| $1,000 | $1,080 | $1,000 | $1,000 |

Threshold waiver creates a $79 downward step between $399 and $400; creditable minimum avoids it. Credits in this example expire each month and are not carried forward; if carried, track liability and reduce future revenue, never count twice. "Waived after X deals" has similar incentives to split/combine jobs; dollar credit is simpler.

A maximum total burden must state denominator and period: e.g. monthly base plus commissions cannot exceed an agreed acquisition-cost budget for eligible collected work. If the cap is zero in a no-deal month, the base is not a guaranteed floor. Do not advertise both unconditional base funding and zero cost without revenue.

## Recurring contract structures
Compare one-time activation fee; fixed payment at first verified receipt; share of first 6 or 12 months' actual receipts; payment at actual renewal; or retainer for ongoing management. Terms are examples, not recommended durations. Avoid paying upfront against an uncollected annual commitment unless clawbacks and cash risk are understood. Define partial refunds, chargebacks, expansion, cancellation, renewal, customer identity and changed entity ownership.

Small monthly fees can be uneconomic to reconcile individually. An invented $12/month fee with 15 minutes of manual work at $50/hour costs $12.50 in labor before payment costs. A pooled quarterly ledger may help; ignoring the work does not.

## Five/ten-year tails: distinguish four different things
1. Collection tail on already-earned, documented deals.
2. Limited commission on a specifically introduced customer's defined contract/renewals.
3. Fee on every new deal between two named partners, whether stewarded or not.
4. Fee on all future business through descendants of the original relationship.

Each step broadens attribution and reporting burden. The founder's desire to be paid for created value is commercially understandable. It does not establish agreement, collectibility or enforceability.

| Tail design | Commercial case | Strongest objection / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Short run-off of recorded open deals | Prevents artificial timing of payment/exit | Define opportunity list and ordinary sales cycle; not new leads forever |
| 12–24 month declining fee for defined introduced account | Finite acquisition value; possibly substitutes for upfront fee | Ask recipient to price buyout alternative; no assumed legal approval |
| Five-year declining relationship fee | Could finance substantial upfront investment in a scarce channel | Startup partner may refuse audit duties and obligations after operator stops |
| Ten-year fee | Strongest theoretical reward for long-lived creation | Ownership changes, attribution decay, admin cost and unfairness perception compound |
| Perpetual fee | Maximum theoretical upside | Not a default; very hard to bound/evaluate and may create legal restraints |
| Buyout option | Converts uncertain tail into agreed settlement | Formula must not become punitive; recipient may prefer larger setup fee initially |

Illustrative math only: $10,000/year eligible receipts, 3% fee = $300/year. At 20% annual continuation probability loss and 10% discount rate, five-year expected present fee receipts are approximately $792 and ten-year $954 (first-year continuation assumed certain; payments year-end). The second five years add only about $161 before auditing, collections, disputes and legal costs. This is not the expected revenue of any actual relationship. If enforcement/reconciliation costs $100/year while active, subtract those costs on the same survival basis. Long duration alone is not economic protection.

Commercial counteroffer: finite activation price + optional active management + clearly limited run-off or defined-account payment schedule. Permit direct dealings; charge only what was affirmatively agreed for a permissible activity. No secret tracking, retroactive fees, automatic downstream chains or punitive non-circumvention clauses.

## Decision
Hybrid survives as an option, especially a **creditable minimum** or modest base with a credible total-cost cap. It is not inherently better than a retainer or simple win fee. Long-term compensation survives only as a narrow negotiated branch, not as the foundation of operator economics. Do not build the business around enforcing five/ten-year claims against partners who no longer want the service.
