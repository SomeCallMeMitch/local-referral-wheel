# 12 — Test portfolio: what deserves real-world validation

Status: completed 2026-09-13. This file synthesizes Session 03 only; it does not add new market research. No prospects were contacted, no software was built, and no legal/commercial arrangement was approved in this session.

## Decision principle

The next test should validate the hardest assumptions with the smallest network possible. Do **not** recruit a full wheel merely to learn whether two firms can sustain a useful partnership. Do **not** make five/ten-year tails, custom software, or success-fee collection necessary for the first test.

The sequence should test four things in order:

1. Will a real buyer pay for the operator's partnership work?
2. Can two complementary firms create a real, permissioned handoff under a sponsor + champion operating model?
3. Is there useful operator work after the relationship is activated, or should the engagement end successfully?
4. Can delivery remain useful when the founder is not personally performing every operating step?

This ordering follows the historical reconstruction, scenario stress tests, fatal-flaw analysis, operator economics, and the outside-the-box work. See [01](01_ORIGINAL_MODEL_RECONSTRUCTION.md), [04](04_SCENARIO_STRESS_TESTS.md), [05](05_FATAL_FLAWS_AND_MITIGATIONS.md), [09](09_OPERATOR_ECONOMICS.md), and [11](11_OUTSIDE_THE_BOX_MODELS.md).

# TEST NOW

## 1. Partnership Transition / Activation Service — **TEST FIRST**

This combines the strongest new Session 03 concept with the finite activation-sprint logic. It is deliberately smaller than a wheel.

**Hypothesis**  
A B2B firm with an existing, dormant, founder-dependent, or underperforming complementary partnership will pay for a finite project that installs sponsor/champion ownership, identifies referral triggers, establishes a permissioned handoff process, and gets the partnership to a live operating state. At least one real handoff can occur without requiring both founders to personally run every step.

**Smallest real-world test**  
Use one paying buyer and one existing or already-interested complementary partner. Reconstruct why the relationship exists, name an executive sponsor and operating champion on each side, define 3–5 concrete customer triggers, agree a simple permissioned introduction workflow, and operate it for 30–45 days. Use existing email/docs/CRM or a controlled sheet. No custom software and no transaction commission are required for this test.

**Participants required**
- 1 paying B2B company;
- 1 complementary partner company;
- ideally 1 sponsor + 1 operating champion at the buyer;
- at least 1 empowered contact at the partner;
- founder/operator as facilitator.

The minimum functional network is two firms, not a six- or eight-member wheel.

**Time required**  
4–6 calendar weeks; plan roughly 12–20 operator hours for discovery, setup, two working reviews, live follow-up, and closeout. These are test budgets, not validated delivery times.

**Cash required**  
Approximately $0–$250 of operating cash using existing tools, excluding founder time and any legal/contract review chosen before a paid engagement. Do not spend on custom software, paid lead sources, large events, or branding infrastructure for this test.

**Success criteria**
- the buyer agrees to a real fixed project fee rather than only a free experiment;
- both firms name accountable owners and complete the operating setup;
- at least one genuine permissioned customer opportunity or equally concrete live trigger is processed during the test;
- the operating champion can complete at least one cycle without the founder personally doing every action;
- both sides can explain what the operator changed that they were not reliably doing before;
- the project either ends as a useful completed engagement or produces an explicit, separately purchased need for ongoing operations.

A successful finite project is a success even if no retainer is needed afterward.

**Failure / kill criteria**
- appropriately matched firms will discuss partnerships but no plausible buyer will pay for the finite work;
- sponsors will not give champions authority or time to act;
- the pair has no real customer-overlap triggers despite apparently complementary services;
- the process produces only generic networking conversations rather than permissioned customer situations;
- the founder must personally create every opportunity and every handoff, with no evidence of a workable sponsor/champion transfer;
- participants say the setup was useful but clearly reproducible with trivial existing effort and would not buy it again in another partnership.

**What this resolves**  
Buyer willingness, transferability of the historical mechanism, delegation risk, whether activation itself is a sellable product, and whether recurring service is actually necessary.

## 2. Single-Buyer Partner Portfolio Management — **TEST SECOND WHEN CONTINUING WORK EXISTS**

Session 03 concept D remains the strongest recurring-service configuration because one buyer is clear, direct partner communication is allowed, and the operator is paid for work rather than ownership of every downstream deal.

**Hypothesis**  
One B2B company will pay a recurring retainer to build and operate a small portfolio of complementary referral/channel relationships, and the ongoing workload remains valuable after initial introductions.

**Smallest real-world test**  
Preferably graduate from a successful activation project. Expand one buyer from one working partner to 2–3 complementary partners and operate the portfolio for 60 days. Track trigger reviews, introductions, next actions, capacity, dropped handoffs, partner issues, and replacement/gap work. Charge for the operating service itself; do not require success fees to make the first test economic.

**Participants required**
- 1 paying buyer;
- 1 executive sponsor and 1 operating champion at that buyer where feasible;
- 2–3 partner firms with named contacts;
- founder/operator.

**Time required**  
8 weeks; provisional 25–40 operator hours including expansion/setup. Replace these assumptions with actual time logs.

**Cash required**  
Approximately $50–$300 using existing tools, excluding founder time and any contract/legal review. No custom software.

**Success criteria**
- buyer pays an actual monthly retainer or a clearly separated ongoing-management fee;
- at least two partner relationships remain active enough to require real coordination during the test;
- the operator can point to continuing work beyond the original introduction: trigger review, handoff recovery, capacity/gap management, replacement, escalation, or partner development;
- buyer requests continuation, expansion, or a lower maintenance tier based on that work;
- actual contribution/hour is acceptable against the founder's chosen earnings floor after all sales, follow-up, travel, admin, and dispute time;
- direct communication between buyer and partners does not destroy the value proposition.

**Failure / kill criteria**
- after activation the buyer and partners operate equally well without continued help and do not want to pay for maintenance;
- the retainer is sustained only by inventing meetings, reports, or reminders that do not change outcomes;
- actual operator workload makes an affordable retainer uneconomic;
- the buyer expects the operator to become an unlicensed salesperson, negotiate customer contracts, or own unrelated agency work;
- delivery still depends on founder persuasion that cannot be transferred or standardized.

**What this resolves**  
Whether there is a durable recurring service, actual workload economics, buyer concentration risk, and whether this can become an outsourced partner-operations business rather than a membership organization.

## 3. Referral Recovery / Handoff Reliability Service — **TEST WHEN A REAL QUEUE ALREADY EXISTS**

Session 03 concept I and the newly invented referral-recovery desk are the cleanest test when a firm already has partner-originated opportunities but is losing them operationally.

**Hypothesis**  
A firm with existing referral/partner flow has enough stale, dropped, or poorly owned handoffs to pay for a workload-bounded service that monitors the queue, escalates exceptions, reconciles outcomes, and protects source/customer trust.

**Smallest real-world test**  
Begin with a short audit of one firm's recent partner-originated handoffs. If there is a meaningful queue, run a 30-day paid exception-management pilot using the client's existing systems or a controlled shared ledger. Do not recruit a new network merely to create work for the service.

**Participants required**
- 1 paying firm;
- 1 sponsor/manager;
- 1 internal operating owner;
- 1–3 existing referral-source or recipient relationships represented in the queue;
- founder/operator.

**Time required**  
1–2 weeks for audit/definition plus 30 days of operation; provisional 12–24 operator hours depending on queue size.

**Cash required**  
Approximately $0–$150 using existing tools, excluding founder time and any legal/privacy review required for access to customer/referral records.

**Success criteria**
- the pre-test audit finds enough actionable handoffs to justify a paid operating service rather than a one-time cleanup;
- buyer pays for the pilot;
- stale items are measurably resolved, re-owned, closed, declined, or escalated rather than merely receiving more reminders;
- the buyer values the exception report / recovered customer-care process enough to renew or expand it;
- actual workload can be bounded and priced without depending on a percentage of customer revenue;
- service can be delivered with appropriate data permissions and named internal accountability.

**Failure / kill criteria**
- too few actionable handoffs exist to support a recurring service;
- a simple CRM view, one staff procedure, or finite cleanup solves the problem adequately;
- the payer will not grant enough access/authority for the operator to resolve exceptions;
- the operator becomes a reminder service while internal owners continue ignoring the work;
- privacy, customer-consent, or sector rules make the needed workflow impractical.

**What this resolves**  
Whether operational leakage itself is a buyable recurring problem, whether the work can be measured independently of causal revenue claims, and whether this service is easier to scale than creating referral demand.

# DROP FOR NOW

These are not declared impossible. They do not deserve scarce validation time now.

| Model / assumption | Why drop it now |
|---|---|
| **C — Success-fee-only wheel as the default business** | Operator bankrolls dry periods, depends on voluntary reporting/collection, invites bypass, and may require higher fees than recipients can afford. Keep success fees only as a later pricing branch when supply and compliance are already known |
| **Broad five/ten-year or perpetual relationship royalty** | Attribution decays while audit, collection, trust, ownership-change, and legal questions grow. Near-term economics must work without it |
| **Large 40–50 company general networking group** | Contrary to the observed mechanism and founder preference; adds weak adjacency and meeting/admin cost before a two-firm mechanism is proven |
| **Regulated transaction-fee wheel spanning mortgage/title, legal, healthcare, insurance, or similar sectors** | Compensation/activity boundaries are too restrictive for a first test; ordinary B2B partner operations is the cleaner starting point |
| **Home-improvement commission wheel as first test** | Attractive ticket sizes do not establish contribution margin or permissible compensation; contractor/HIS boundaries need exact review |
| **Transaction/move commission wheel as first test** | Event adjacency is real in principle, but real-estate/settlement compensation constraints and incumbent vendor relationships make it an unnecessarily complicated first validation |
| **AI-first or custom-SaaS-first business** | Software cannot manufacture partner intent, trust, permission, capacity, or demand. Existing tools are sufficient to validate the human workflow |
| **Territory/franchise expansion before delegated operation works** | Historical evidence already warns that founder-to-subordinate transfer can break referral flow |

# Recommended test sequence

Do **one test at a time**.

1. **Paid Partnership Transition / Activation test with one B2B pair.**
2. If the pair becomes self-sustaining, record a successful finite product and test the offer with another pair rather than forcing a retainer.
3. If the buyer still has valuable ongoing partner work, graduate to **Single-Buyer Partner Portfolio Management**.
4. If the observed pain is specifically dropped existing handoffs, branch to **Referral Recovery / Handoff Reliability** instead.
5. Only after repeated relationship flow is observed should a small **hybrid wheel** be tested against the simpler retainer/finite alternatives.
6. Do not test custom software, long post-exit tails, or multi-city expansion until the human operating model and delegation have passed.

## Session 03 portfolio conclusion

The Referral Wheel mechanism remains credible enough to test because the founder reports actual historical deals. The evidence does **not** justify starting with a membership network. The highest-information, lowest-complexity test is a paid finite B2B partnership transition/activation engagement. Its result should determine which recurring business, if any, deserves the next test.
