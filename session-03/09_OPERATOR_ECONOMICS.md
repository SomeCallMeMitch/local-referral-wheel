# 09 — Operator economics and capacity
2026-09-13. **All inputs are invented planning assumptions, not founder numbers, local quotes or validated prices.** Units are one wheel for B/C, one buyer portfolio for D, one firm queue for I, and one anchor account for G. Do not pretend these units have identical acquisition difficulty.

Currency USD. No software sales, licensing or five/ten-year fee tails included. Recipient affordability is a separate mandatory gate; operator profitability alone is insufficient.

## Churn shock
One of four B recipients leaves: assume base drops 25% ($60) and wins/qualified volume drop 25%, while fixed work stays. Revenue becomes $180+3×$300=$1,080; hours 12+12×.4+3×.5=18.3; contribution $847.60, or $46.32/hour before replacement effort. Add six replacement hours and return falls to $34.88/hour. Source loss could remove far more than 25% of wins.

For D/I/G losing the sole buyer/anchor is a 100% revenue loss for that unit until replaced. One month of lost D billing with $200 overhead is negative $200 cash contribution, plus replacement sales hours. Across five equal units, one full-month account loss removes 20% of billing, not necessarily 20% of work.

## Cash lag and break-even
B/C win assumptions represent matured collected outcomes. A signed $25,000 customer contract is not operator cash. If first fees arrive in month 3, initial overhead and all operator work still occur; hybrid base partly offsets them but may not cover service. Track monthly cash cohort rather than claiming a steady-state month from day one.

For C with Q=4W: H=12+2.7W, cash contribution=.97×450W−200. Cover $50/hour:
436.5W−200 >=600+135W → W>=2.65 collected wins/month, requiring about 10.6 qualified introductions at the assumed 25% rate. This excludes setup/capital costs and assumes each $450 fee is affordable and permissible.

For B with Q=4W: H=12+2.1W, cash contribution=.97×(240+300W)−200.
32.8+291W >=600+105W → W>=3.05, Q>=12.2/month. Crediting bases against fees would reduce revenue vs this additive case and require recalculation by payer, not aggregate max across the whole group.

## When unattractive
No credible recipient-fee budget; recurring contribution below the chosen floor; queues exceed capacity; collection delays exceed funded runway; staff utilization too low; growth requires more owner persuasion than can be delegated; or the model only looks profitable with unearned tails. D/I have simpler revenue but require an actual buyer willing to fund the work. B/C require measured qualified supply, affordable fees and low leakage. No model has passed these tests yet.
