## Why delegation may have failed
| Hypothesis | Distinguishing observation | Design around it | Cost/remaining risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authority lost | Staff saw need but could not approve introductions | Executive sponsor approves boundaries; named operational owner | Sponsor still must respond |
| Incentives mismatched | Referrals not in staff goals; reward stayed with owner | Employer sets legitimate internal goals/recognition; avoid external employee kickbacks | Competes with core selling goals |
| Client knowledge lost | Delegate lacked account context | Include appropriate account owner in trigger review | Privacy and time |
| Trust stayed with founders | Clients/partners reluctant with new contact | Joint transition and sponsor endorsement | Cannot automate credibility |
| Accountability unclear | Each thought other person owned follow-up | Single responsible person and next-action date | Requires management reinforcement |
| Speed declined | Longer acknowledgment/approval delays | Escalation after agreed response interval | Operator becomes chaser |
| No workflow | Opportunities remembered only in meetings | Simple account-review prompt + event log | CRM alone cannot create intent |
| Lower priority/ownership | Staff attended but brought no opportunities | Review sponsor objectives monthly; pause unproductive partnership | May remain founder-dependent |
| Opportunity pool exhausted | Few fresh suitable clients even under original owners | Compare client cohort/new-customer inflow before/after | Means structural frequency problem |
| Seasonality or business changes | Decline coincided with weaker demand/capacity | Compare underlying business trends | Small sample cannot isolate cause |
| Referral fatigue/service disappointment | Fewer recommendations after bad experience | Private quality interviews and complaint review | May require replacement rather than reminders |

Do not conclude "owners must always attend." Test a sponsor-plus-champion structure: executive sponsor retains authority, named champion operates workflow, short sponsor review checks outcomes. Observe whether qualified opportunities and service standards survive a planned handoff. A single failed handoff is a reason to investigate, not a universal rule.

## Transferability stress
E-commerce services can have high recurring contract value and founders deeply engaged with clients. Local project businesses can have thinner contribution, more subcontract/material costs, less concentrated client knowledge and licensing constraints. The more promising transfer may be another complementary B2B service pair, even if it is not a traditional local wheel.

## What to reconstruct when evidence is available
Anonymized relationship IDs; actual start/stop dates; sponsors/champions; shared client type; referrals by direction; accepted/signed/paid dates; fee basis and cash; operator actions/hours; direct recipient costs; attribution disputes; transition dates and baseline client activity. No historical amount is filled in without records. A small number of redacted examples is enough to improve judgment; no need to gather whole client databases.

## Current conclusion
Real founder-reported transactions increase confidence that the mechanism can work. The central unproven business is **reliably operating that mechanism when the owner is not personally doing every part**. Selling relationship activation and handoff reliability may be stronger than selling membership or a long claim on all future business.
