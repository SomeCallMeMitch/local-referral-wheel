# 06 — Four local architectures
2026-09-13. Vallejo/Benicia first; neighboring service areas only where providers actually overlap. Research supports category presence, not referral demand or margins. Financial ranges below are hypothetical screening cases, not local averages. See 07 for counted public candidates and limitations.

## Ranking
1. **Local B2B operations pair/cluster**: best match to historical mechanism and connector role; slower sales but potentially repeat account contact.
2. **Property-owner service coordination**: repeated needs and a possible anchor payer; conflicts/licensed scope need care.
3. **Home-improvement wheel**: intuitive adjacency, but irregular project flow and contractor-fee questions.
4. **Transaction/move wheel**: event density attractive, compensation boundaries and existing relationships make it a weaker first fee test.

Rank shifts if warm access or actual partner economics contradict these hypotheses. Do not pick industries solely by contract value.

## Designed paths, not random category lists
**B2B:** bookkeeper notices repeated invoicing/admin friction → qualified CRM specialist; CRM specialist encounters security/infrastructure needs → MSP; MSP sees hiring → payroll provider. Verify actual shared client size and service scope. A referral to "any business needing marketing" is not sufficient adjacency. An excellent account source need not buy a seat.

**Property owner:** anchor receives permitted maintenance request → qualified provider; turnover prompts cleaning and repairs. This is often one-to-many procurement, not two-way peer referrals. Anchor-funded coordination may be the correct business, with customer interests and disclosure central.

**Home improvement:** plumber resolves leak → qualified remediation if needed → flooring/painter. Roofer identifies a need for a separate qualified trade only within competence. Do not bundle contracts or imply the connector undertakes work. Roofing and solar may be complementary in some events, but scope/warranty/timing conflicts make them situational rather than automatically ideal partners.

**Move:** customer asks for help preparing space → appropriate cleaning/organizing/painting introductions. Keep professional settlement services outside initial transaction-fee assumptions. A useful customer event does not authorize paid steering.

## Category rules
Protected scope is a narrowly defined in-network matching preference, not market exclusivity. Retain backups for capacity/quality. Two firms sharing "home improvement" may compete materially even when trade labels differ. A CPA, bookkeeper and payroll provider may overlap or be complementary depending on actual services.

Strong source admission test: at least two recent examples of genuine adjacent customer need; named executive sponsor and operating champion; willingness to protect customer consent. Recipient test: wanted job type, capacity, quality evidence and positive contribution after total fees. A nonresponsive firm is not made suitable by paying more.

## Local evidence and uncertainties
Benicia's public chamber directory contains roofing, bookkeeping/payroll, property management and other relevant categories; this establishes an initial screening path. [Directory](https://members.beniciachamber.com/directory). It does not prove independent ownership, willingness, licensing or referrals. Provider service-area pages in 07 confirm some cross-city reach but not low travel cost. Public sources do not establish typical local margins, referral frequency or acquisition budgets; collect these privately in a future authorized test.

## Architecture decision
Explore one B2B pair and one anchor/household service pattern conceptually before recruiting an entire wheel. The smallest functional network might be two excellent complementary firms with sponsor/champion accountability. More categories can dilute focus rather than add value.
