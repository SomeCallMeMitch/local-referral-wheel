Completed 2026-09-13 following founder clarification. Evaluation only. The founder has clarified preferences, not selected a final business or authorized implementation/outreach. Real-world testing remains outside scope. This summary supersedes conflicting preference assumptions in Session 4.

## What changed

The core concept is now much clearer: the founder operates small mutual relationships, has brief weekly conversations, keeps the CRM current and shows pending/closed/paid deals. Participants do not need to use a CRM regularly. An optional lead-submission portal is helpful but not required. Suitable partners should produce commission income in the low four- to five-figure monthly range; monthly fees are not the intended engine.

The founder regards local mutual networks as a likely easiest starting context, has unpaid development time, has not supplied a cash budget, prefers little urgent support or employee management, and likes the possibility of getting a project going, selling it and moving on. A FRAP-like system for other operators is of interest. Exact meaning of selling remains open; do not silently translate it into selling a one-time consulting project.

These corrections move commission-operated networks to the front of current conceptual development. Standalone SaaS remains an independent option from Session 4, but an internal CRM does not need an external SaaS business model or frequent member use to justify its utility.

## Concrete arrangements developed

**Local B2B recurring-service network:** e.g., managed IT, clearly separate CRM/website implementation and ordinary business-administration support. Existing client needs prompt permissioned introductions; brief weekly conversations keep relevant pairings and pending opportunities moving. Receiving firms pay agreed eligible commissions. Larger affordable fees can support meaningful income with fewer wins, but sales cycles, scope overlap, client flow and collection are unknown. Regulated professional services are not assumed cleared.

**Planned home-service network:** e.g., painting, flooring and organizing/cleaning with genuine local service overlap. The founder coordinates introductions for planned work, while providers handle quoting, scheduling and service problems. This is tangible and locally oriented, but smaller fee budgets can require more transactions and contractor arrangements need precise activity/compensation screening. It is not emergency dispatch.

**Portfolio of selected pairs:** maintain two or three productive relationships, each with separate short conversations where useful, instead of making every provider attend one group. Each recipient pays its eligible fee. This permits concentrated relationship work and possible diversification without employees, but common sources can correlate income and founder trust may not transfer.

These are operating/market configurations of the same commission business, not three new businesses distinguished only by price. Full examples, weekly role, monthly economics, failure cases and sale implications are in [concrete networks](session-05/01_CONCRETE_COMMISSION_NETWORKS.md).

No purchases are being made. Personal living-cost runway remains unknown.

## FRAP and selling something

The old repository contained only a brief FRAP treatment. Its old linked page did not load; current official chapter and certification pages were reviewed. They show a packaged leader model with training, license, software and support; public revenue examples use member fees. That supports analyzing an operator-system business, not a claim that FRAP independently validates demand for this commission concept. Promotional earnings and tax claims are not adopted. [FRAP opportunity](https://frapchapters.com/), [certification offer](https://frapchapters.com/pif).

Three different sale products are now separated:

1. **Sell the operating network:** another owner takes over weekly partner work and eligible commissions. Value depends on transferable agreements, records, partner confidence and earnings after replacing the founder—not the CRM alone. This most closely matches building an operation then moving on, pending clarification.
2. **Sell/license an operator system:** other connectors buy an original operating method, tool and bounded training/support; they operate their networks. Possible upfront package pricing or a share of their commissions need not impose membership dues on participating firms. This best matches the FRAP analogy, but shifts work into operator sales/support.
3. **Sell a finite setup service:** a participating business pays for a completed partnership/process installation. This is a project sale, not a business sale, and must not become the default over the commission preference.

Full cash distinctions, primary sources and hypothetical buyer/license economics are in [cash, FRAP and sale analysis](session-05/02_CASH_FRAP_AND_SELLING_THE_BUSINESS.md).
