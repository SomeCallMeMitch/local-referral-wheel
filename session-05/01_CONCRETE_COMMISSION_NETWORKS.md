# Session 5 — Concrete commission-network versions

2026-09-13. Hypothetical business design only. Every amount, conversion, workload and partner arrangement below is invented. No actual companies are committed or being approached. The purpose is to show what the businesses would feel like to own.

## The common business, now stated concretely

You bring together a few complementary businesses with customer overlap. You learn what each wants, hold short weekly conversations with the most relevant pairings, surface suitable customer needs, help introductions happen, follow pending opportunities and record results. The receiving business pays an agreed commission on eligible outcomes. Your private CRM lets you track and show the work; partners can supply a lead through a simple portal, email or the weekly conversation.

The customer does business directly with the provider. You maintain the partnership and referral process rather than performing the provider's work. Routine direct communication is welcome. You need information about commission eligibility and collection, not ownership of every conversation.

This is an actively operated, commission-funded partnership business. It does not need membership fees, workshops, frequent partner logins, emergency dispatch or employees in its small-owner-operated form. Whether its commissions are large and frequent enough is the central economic question.

The first three versions below are distinct operating/market configurations of that core business—not three new businesses invented by changing the price. The later sale/license possibilities are different products or ownership paths, developed in document 02.

## Version 1: A small local B2B recurring-services network

**Who is in it?** For illustration, a managed-IT firm, a CRM/website implementation firm, and an ordinary business-administration service that helps clients with back-office processes. Their scopes must genuinely differ. An IT firm that already sells the CRM work is not automatically a suitable noncompeting partner. Accounting, tax, insurance or other regulated advice is not assumed included or cleared by an administrative label.

**What creates referrals?** A business-administration provider learns that a client is manually retyping customer information and losing follow-ups. With the customer's permission, it introduces the CRM specialist. The CRM specialist identifies an unrelated device/security support need and introduces the IT firm when appropriate. The IT firm encounters an owner overwhelmed by administrative onboarding and points them toward the operations service. These are potential needs, not a guaranteed circular flow.

**What you do each week:** briefly review the best active pairing: which client situations actually fit, what introductions are awaiting permission, what referred opportunities are pending and what needs a decision. You enter updates. You might have one three-party conversation while small, then separate pair conversations where customer overlap warrants them. No requirement that every provider sends to every other provider weekly.

**What generates income?** A new collected contract for a receiving provider triggers a defined commission. Hypothetical: a $24,000 first-year service contract has $15,000 delivery/selling/reserve costs and requires $6,500 retained contribution, leaving $2,500 as the maximum fee budget. A $1,500 commission fits that illustration and leaves $7,500 contribution after the fee. It does not establish that any actual provider has these economics or would pay that fee. Annual contract value is not money collected at signing.

**Why the partners care:** appropriate introductions from firms their clients already trust, with someone making partner attention and follow-up habitual. Sources can gain better client care and reciprocal opportunity over time; equal monthly wins are unnecessary. Customers must still benefit from the recommendation itself.

**Why this could suit you:** several substantial commissions could support meaningful income without managing many small transactions. Planned conversations fit a low-urgency role. It resembles the historical e-commerce-service mechanism while allowing local relationships.

**What could defeat it:** long sales cycles, low new-client flow, vendors already overlapping, little need for each other's services, or an unwillingness to report eligible receipts. A quiet partnership does not become productive just because the meeting is weekly. Existing customers receiving a new related service may offer quicker opportunities than establishing a wholly new source, but neither timing is known here.

**What an ordinary month might look like:** 12 suitable introductions, three collected eligible wins at an average $1,500 = $4,500 gross commission; at modeled $200 cash overhead and 3% processing, $4,165 before personal tax and owner labor. A different month could have introductions but no collected commissions. These are mature-month possibilities, not first-month forecasts.

**What could be sold later:** an operating portfolio with documented commission arrangements and transferable partner confidence. Selling the CRM alone is a different product and does not sell the relationships. The buyer would need to be capable of maintaining the weekly role, and transfer cannot be assumed from a spreadsheet of contact names.

## Version 2: A planned home-improvement relationship network

**Who is in it?** A painting company, a flooring company and a home-organizing or recurring-cleaning business serving an overlapping Vallejo/Benicia area. Optional fourth partner could be another genuinely complementary, planned-work service. Do not add a roofing or emergency-restoration company merely because its ticket size looks attractive.

**What creates referrals?** A cleaner hears that an owner is planning a room refresh. The owner agrees to an introduction to the painter. During the discussion the owner asks about replacing flooring; the painter introduces the flooring firm after permission. Separately, a flooring client might need help reorganizing rooms. Some sources will be much more productive than others; the organizer or cleaner should not be forced to receive equal deal dollars.

**What you do:** short weekly conversations on upcoming planned projects and pending introductions; occasional joint conversation between painter and flooring firm where their sequencing matters. You check that introductions were acknowledged and track quotes/wins. Scheduling crews, estimating construction work, resolving installation defects and after-hours dispatch are not part of the contemplated connector role.

**What generates income?** Defined commissions from receiving providers on eligible work, only under an actually permissible arrangement. Illustrative $8,000 job: costs/reserve $5,800 and required retained contribution $1,400 leave a fee budget of $800. A $500 fee fits that job. A $2,000 job with only $150 of affordable acquisition surplus does not support the same fee. A five-figure customer invoice does not automatically support a four-figure commission.

**Why partners care:** relevant local introductions while preserving direct customer relationships. You may make it easier to remember an appropriate partner and keep a potential deal from being forgotten. The source's reputation is exposed to the receiving firm's service quality.

**Why this could suit you:** familiar local businesses and tangible opportunities may make this easier to understand and organize. That is consistent with your preference, but not verified evidence of easier acquisition. Planned work fits better than urgent repairs with your support preference.

**What could defeat it:** small fee budgets require high volume; sources repeat similar client lists; everyone gets busy simultaneously; contractor compensation/activity restrictions may limit the exact arrangement. California BPC 7157 contains relevant restrictions and exceptions; it does not establish blanket permission or a blanket ban on every conceivable introduction arrangement. This branch remains conditional on its exact participants, conduct and compensation. [California statute](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=7157.).

**Illustrative month:** eight eligible collected wins at $500 = $4,000 commissions; modeled cash contribution $3,680. Reaching $10,000 gross would require 20 such wins. That difference makes this a materially more transaction-heavy version than high-fee B2B, even with equally brief meetings.

**What could be sold later:** the operating local referral business and its transferable arrangements, where permitted and accepted—not ownership of customers or compulsory future referrals. A prospective operator would care about source concentration and whether partner trust survives a change in coordinator.

## Version 3: Several small partner pairs managed as one portfolio

**Who is in it?** Instead of one group in which everyone must fit, you maintain two or three strong pairings. For example, IT ↔ CRM implementation, commercial cleaning ↔ office move support, and a separate planned-work local pair. These are possible pair types, not claims of actual supply or cleared fees. Avoid a regulated or thin-margin pair merely to complete the portfolio.

**What creates referrals?** Each pair has its own customer triggers. You hold separate conversations because the relationships have different client needs; no general networking meeting is required. Some businesses may appear in multiple pairs, but then their value is correlated rather than diversified.

**What you do:** a 10–20-minute weekly conversation per active pair, record pending/closed outcomes, coordinate relevant introductions and periodically rethink an inactive pairing. If two firms have little to discuss, shorten or alter cadence rather than manufacture activity. The founder's proposed separate meetings become a deliberate operating architecture.

**What generates income?** Each receiving firm pays the applicable eligible-deal fee; there is no portfolio retainer in the core scenario. Three independent pairs each generating two $1,000 commissions in a mature month produce $6,000 gross, or $5,620 after illustrative $200/3% cash costs. If only one pair produces, gross is $2,000 and contribution $1,740. Independence and source reliability matter more than the number of logos.

**Why partners care:** concentrated attention to a specific productive relationship rather than meeting unrelated businesses. You do not require them to treat every contact as a networking obligation.

**Why this could suit you:** more control over meeting time, no need for all-to-all fit, and potential diversification without employees. It also creates a unit you could conceive of transferring to another operator. It is still the same ongoing relationship business, not a different business because the diagram changed.

**What could defeat it:** a few pairs have correlated quiet periods, context switching grows, or each pair depends on your personal involvement. Four weekly conversations are light; forty are a different job. If a pair requires no ongoing work after a successful introduction, retaining that pair's revenue needs an agreed, affordable and permissible earned-fee basis rather than a fictional service.

**What could be sold later:** the entire portfolio or an agreed transferable subset. A partial sale must explain who handles shared partners and existing eligible opportunities. Otherwise you sell a conflict rather than an independently operable asset.

## Brief weekly conversations and the CRM

Illustrative 15-minute agenda: five minutes of relevant client situations, five minutes of pending introductions, five minutes on next actions/obstacles. It may take less, and some weeks may be asynchronous. This is an example cadence, not a prescribed program to implement now.

With three partners you could hold one short joint conversation where useful. With more partners, schedule by active relationship rather than every possible pairing. Six firms have 15 possible pairings; you might need only three or four relevant conversations, not 15 weekly meetings. Four 15-minute conversations per week are about 4.33 hours/month; adding five minutes of preparation/update per conversation brings that to about 5.77 hours. Referrals, reconciliation and new partner work are additional.

**Your internal view:** source, recipient, permission/contact boundary, next owner/action, pending/accepted/quoted/won/lost, customer payment status, eligible fee, commission invoiced/paid and notes. Separate won, customer paid and your commission paid. These are different events.

**What partners see:** a private filtered report for their own opportunities—pending items, actions needed and eligible outcomes. No shared disclosure of other firms' private client lists or deal economics. A lead portal can be a short optional submission form; sending you an email or mentioning a lead during a call is not inferior if you can record it reliably.

**Where software adds optional benefit:** simple lead submission, a clear pending-deal report, approved reminders and a concise weekly recap. AI can draft notes for your review. No partner habit of opening a dashboard is required. Reporting usefulness supports your service; it is not proof of independent SaaS demand.

**The real data dependency:** you can enter the record, but you cannot know an unreported cash receipt or determine a disputed eligible commission through interface design. A regular brief status reconciliation is more relevant than partner login frequency. If even that small cooperation is absent, outcome-paid economics weaken.

## Commission income: what the target implies

The founder's range is not an exact target. Use $3,000, $6,000 and $10,000 as planning points for monthly owner cash contribution **before personal tax and without deducting a fictional salary paid to yourself**. This is not after-tax take-home income.

Illustrative cash allowance $200/month plus 3% payment/collection-processing cost. Neither is a quote or required purchase. Required collected wins = ceiling((target+$200)/(.97×average actually collected fee)). No monthly dues assumed. If fees are charged differently or collected at low bank-transfer cost, change the allowance.

| Average collected commission per win | Wins for $3,000 | Wins for $6,000 | Wins for $10,000 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $250 | 14 | 26 | 43 |
| $750 | 5 | 9 | 15 |
| $1,500 | 3 | 5 | 8 |
| $2,500 | 2 | 3 | 5 |

This does not imply larger fees can simply be chosen. They must fit recipient economics and the arrangement. For small commissions, transaction volume becomes the challenge. At meaningful $1,500–2,500 commissions, a handful of wins can support the desired range, but contract values, actual margins, suitable source flow, close rate and collection timing must all cooperate.

At a hypothetical 25% qualified-introduction-to-collected-win rate, five wins need 20 qualified introductions and eight wins need 32. At 10%, they need 50 and 80. These are conversions across comparable cohorts, not necessarily leads entered and wins collected in the same calendar month. Four active source relationships supplying five suitable introductions/month each would make 20; one each would make four. The weekly meeting itself does not supply the customer need.

## Can it happen quickly?

Yes as a possible situation: partners already have trust, current customers with suitable needs, reasonable sales cycles, and agreed collectible commissions. Two $2,500 fees would create $5,000 gross as soon as those eligible outcomes actually pay. One strong relationship could outperform several weak ones. That is consistent with the founder's historical report of substantial fees, not a forecast for the next arrangement.

Compare three invented timing cases using $1,500 fees:

- **Fast:** three eligible deals pay in month one → $4,500 gross and $4,165 modeled contribution.
- **Collection lag:** the same three deals are won in month one but commission cash arrives in month three → months one/two can each have −$200 contribution if the allowance is spent; month three has $4,165. Nothing became less valuable merely because the CRM said pending, but cash timing changed.
- **Monthly receipts:** the three customers generate $150 monthly eligible commission each under an agreed schedule → $450 gross/month, not a $4,500 lump sum. Twenty active such accounts create $3,000 gross before overhead; 40 create $6,000; 67 create $10,050. Customer cancellation and eligible duration change the stock of paying accounts.

A lump-sum fee, an agreed percentage of collected invoices and a finite installment commission are alternative structures. Do not count an initial acquisition fee plus all future recurring receipts unless the agreement actually includes both. None assumes indefinite claims after the operator leaves.

## Workload without employees or urgent support

Invented monthly allowance for four active relationships: about six hours of conversations/preparation, four hours of routine review/admin and six hours of partner development/replacement buffer =16 fixed hours. Add 15 minutes per qualified introduction and 15 minutes per collected win for handling/reconciliation. At Q20/W5, total 22.25 hours; at $1,500/win, $7,075 modeled cash contribution, around $318/hour. That attractive result depends on five substantial wins and genuinely low handling time; it is not an expected hourly wage.

At Q20/W1, the same structure takes 21.25 hours and contributes $1,255, about $59/hour. At Q20/W0, 21 hours and −$200 cash contribution. If each introduction takes an hour rather than 15 minutes, Q20/W5 needs 37.25 hours, before an exceptional partner dispute. Initial relationship-building may take an invented 20–60 hours or much more; it is not hidden inside mature monthly income.

Urgency is partly a product choice: planned B2B/customer introductions can leave delivery scheduling and emergencies with the provider. It cannot eliminate occasional problems, but there is no reason to make you a dispatch center. Expansion can mean selling a transferable operation or supporting independent operators rather than hiring coordinators. Those paths bring their own sale/support work, developed next.

## Current conditional development emphasis

The local commission-operated network rises above retainer service and standalone SaaS as the closest fit with the clarified preferences—not as a proven final selection. Develop the B2B recurring-service and selected-pair versions alongside the planned home-service version. The first two have a more plausible arithmetic path to meaningful income with fewer transactions; the home-service version may feel easier locally but needs enough affordable, permissible commissions to avoid volume-heavy work.

The decisive comparison now is not which has the best dashboard. It is which arrangement can generate a small number of sufficiently valuable, collectible commissions while preserving recipient profit and the brief, nonurgent operator role.
