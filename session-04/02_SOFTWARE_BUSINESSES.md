# Session 4 — Software as a full business branch

2026-09-13. Conceptual products, not implementation specifications or build approval. All proposed prices, workload and economics are invented. Software can be evaluated and potentially operated as a standalone company without first running a service network. Existing customers' own relationships can supply the activity a tool supports.

## Eight credible product concepts

These are alternatives, not eight modules to build together. Single-business partner management and a handoff bridge overlap, but the first organizes one team's partner activity while the second coordinates a particular transaction across organizational boundaries. Operator, group and association editions can share technology; their buyers, budgets and participation problems warrant separate evaluation. Multi-location continuity is more than a location-priced copy if its core job is organizational transfer and governance.

### 1. Partner workspace for one business

**User/payer/purchase trigger:** owner, account manager or business-development employee uses it; the company pays after partner commitments and next actions become scattered. Suitable for a firm with several real partner relationships, not someone wanting the app to invent customers.

**Example workflow:** an IT-services company has eight complementary partners. Its account manager opens a weekly list: one partner's sponsor changed, two commitments are due, and a customer request needs a qualified CRM specialist. The manager reviews fit, asks permission, records the introduction and assigns the follow-up. The business can receive useful reminders and ownership history even if only its own staff use the tool. External responses enrich rather than unlock all value.

**Standalone and combined value:** standalone is a focused partner process without buying outsourced management. With a portfolio service, it is the shared operating record; the manager's relationship work is separately valuable. Neither deployment requires a chapter, membership dues or transaction fee.

**Substitutes/switch reason:** HubSpot tasks/deals, spreadsheets, email and broader PRM products are credible substitutes [E1/E4](04_EVIDENCE_AND_RESEARCH.md). Switch only if relationship-level ownership, reusable trigger reviews and easy external acknowledgment save meaningful effort beyond configuration of those tools. A directory with reminders is not enough.

**Data/integrations/participation:** partner profiles, scope, sponsor, owner, commitments and minimal consented opportunity metadata. CSV and selected forwarded messages can be sufficient; optional CRM sync reduces duplicate entry. Internal users must keep owners and next actions current. Do not demand a whole customer database or a partner login for internal task value.

**Automation/humans:** automate due dates, stale-owner alerts and draft summaries. Humans judge fit, permission, reputation and whether continued contact is useful. An AI status summary must cite the record and retain unknown outcomes.

**Economics/distribution/retention:** $49–149/month/company, example $99; potentially reached through CRM consultants, educational search or a vertical software channel. These are hypothetical acquisition mechanisms, not actions scheduled here. At low price, lengthy demonstrations or bespoke CRM mappings destroy economics. Assume 0.25 support hour/company/month and $10 variable infrastructure; 0.97×99−10−$35×0.25 = $77.28 unit contribution before product costs and acquisition. Renewal depends on repeated use by a team, not logins alone. Development complexity moderate: narrow workflow, permissions, imports and messaging; robust sync adds scope.

**Failure/defense:** internal CRM does enough; founder is the sole user and stops; few partner changes; customers demand a full CRM. Defensibility could be a focused workflow in a specific vertical and dependable integration, not exclusive ownership of contact data. Attractive if it stays useful with one paying team's participation; weaker if every partner must join.

### 2. Cross-company handoff receipt and exception tool

**User/payer/purchase trigger:** company with existing introductions, or its partner-operations team, pays for missing acknowledgment and uncertain next ownership. Sending and receiving staff use limited views. One side pays; external counterparts can respond without becoming paid subscribers.

**Workflow:** a business-services firm makes a permissioned introduction. Sender records the intended recipient and scope. Recipient receives a time-limited, identity-checked action link, accepts or declines, names an owner and indicates the next step. Sender sees acknowledgment and agreed outcome status, not the recipient's private sales notes. A stale acknowledgment is an exception; a customer considering an offer is not automatically neglect. Each company keeps its own CRM.

**Standalone/combined:** it supplies a shared receipt and escalation record even without a managed service. A human recovery desk can handle exceptions for customers who outsource that work. Value comes from cross-company status and less re-entry, not guaranteeing more referrals or knowing every closed sale.

**Substitutes/switch:** email/CRM forms, ticketing and referral platforms. Referral Rock already advertises one-click access and integrations [E2](04_EVIDENCE_AND_RESEARCH.md); passwordless interaction is not unique. The proposed difference is minimal mutually shared state across independent firms and explicit responsibility, with no migration. Whether incumbents already solve this adequately is the highest-priority product research question.

**Data/integrations/participation:** referral ID, named permitted recipient, consent evidence, owner, timestamps, status provenance and minimal customer contact. Links need appropriate identity/access protection; forwarded links must not reveal unrestricted customer data. Selected email/form input can start the conceptual workflow; two-way CRM integrations increase maintenance. Recipient response is required for confirmed external status. Missing responses must stay unknown, never inferred closed or failed.

**Automation/humans:** reminders, duplicate checks, escalation rules and draft event extraction; humans resolve complaints, identity ambiguity, consent and disputed outcomes. Source attribution records who introduced whom; it does not establish incremental profit.

**Economics:** $99–299/month/company, $149 example with a defined active-item band. Hypothetical acquisition via CRM implementers or a narrow workflow-specific channel; a $600 fully loaded acquisition/onboarding assumption is used in document 03. Support 0.5 hour/month plus $15 infrastructure yields $112.03 unit contribution at $35/hour support and 3% processing. Retention strongest at frequent handoffs with multiple outside firms; weakest after one-time cleanup. Moderate–high development: access boundaries, delivery reliability, correction history and selected integration. Narrow integrations are materially different from universal email/CRM compatibility.

**Failure/defense:** counterparts do not respond, low volume, link/security friction, CRM does enough, or sensitive status crosses company boundaries. Potential defense is reliable cross-company workflow and distribution through a particular ecosystem; data export should remain possible. It is the strongest standalone software concept for further development under current assumptions, not a claim of a proven market gap.

### 3. Independent relationship-operator workbench

**User/payer/trigger:** independent partnership managers, fractional business-development operators or referral coordinators pay when several client networks become hard to administer. Their clients and partners receive limited reports or action links. Do not assume this is a large population; its size is unresolved.

**Workflow:** an operator starts the morning with exceptions across six client portfolios, drills into one without seeing another client's data, checks stale handoffs, sends approved reminders and produces separate monthly service reports. Relationship health reflects missing owners, capacity changes and unresolved commitments—not a universal partner score.

**Standalone/combined:** operators run their own businesses without our service. A managed network can use the same workbench internally, but internal use is not software sales. Optional onboarding or exception help must have its own labor budget.

**Substitutes/switch:** multiple CRM workspaces, spreadsheets and multi-org referral products, notably Referral Rock's partitioned programs [E3](04_EVIDENCE_AND_RESEARCH.md). A reason to switch is a genuinely faster across-client exception workflow and simple service reporting, not merely multi-tenancy or custom branding.

**Data/integrations/participation:** separate client permissions, partner/relationship IDs, consented handoffs, task ownership, operator work logs and optional agreement metadata. Imports/exports and billing handoff; CRM integration per supported ecosystem. The operator can maintain records centrally, reducing member data-entry burden but adding operator labor. Clients authorize data access; the operator cannot grant rights it lacks.

**Automation/humans:** automate aggregate exception views, report drafts and agreed fee arithmetic. Humans manage trust, interpret imbalances and decide service adjustments. No automatic cross-client data sharing or exploitation of client lists.

**Economics:** $199–499/month/operator, modeled $299, with explicit portfolio limits and paid additional capacity. Acquisition through operator communities, consultants or specialist content is plausible but unmeasured. Assume $1,200 acquisition/onboarding, one support hour/month and $25 variable infrastructure: contribution $230.03/month before fixed product expense. Retention follows the operator's client portfolio; one operator losing several clients can cause a large downgrade. Development high relative to a single workspace because permission errors cross client boundaries.

**Failure/defense:** too few viable professional operators; one operator wants a private custom product; revenue-share licensing leaves the vendor dependent on unverifiable operator sales. A reputation for reliable workflow, templates and operator-focused distribution could help; generic white labeling is weak defense. Strong combined possibility if founder enjoys supporting other connectors rather than operating every relationship personally.

### 4. Existing-group and association follow-through software

**User/payer/trigger:** independent referral-group leaders or chamber/association staff pay from organizational budgets; members submit and update handoffs. The job is completing the follow-up loop in an existing organization, not recruiting a new membership or replacing all membership management.

**Workflow:** a chamber employee records a permissioned member introduction. Recipient confirms contact through a limited response. Staff sees unresolved requests and a renewal report separating introductions, confirmed contacts, unknown outcomes and optionally documented wins. An independent group leader instead starts from member-to-member introductions and assigned follow-up; attendance is secondary.

**Standalone/combined:** the organization's existing staff can operate it without our service. A contracted association coordinator can run exceptions, but SaaS should not require that subsidy. The group edition has lighter budget and governance; the association edition needs reporting boundaries and staff ownership.

**Substitutes/switch:** free/small-price group products and existing association suites. Referral Grove and Referral Hopper show strong price competition; ChamberMaster already records eReferrals [E6–E8](04_EVIDENCE_AND_RESEARCH.md). A case for switching requires demonstrated conceptual workflow advantage in closed-loop follow-through, not a prettier wheel, attendance or directory. An add-on/export-based design may avoid replacing dues, websites and events.

**Data/integrations/participation:** roster IDs, roles and limited handoff records; no mandatory member revenue upload. Roster CSV or a supported membership-system integration; integration availability not assumed. Staff can enter referrals, but members must acknowledge outcomes. Unknown status and opt-outs must remain visible. Avoid publicly shaming low-referral members or implying all referrals are profitable.

**Automation/humans:** digests, private reminders and incomplete-record flags; humans determine appropriate introductions, fairness and sponsor accountability. Software cannot turn a membership roster into active partners.

**Economics:** small-group $29–79/month; organization with real follow-through workflow $149–399/month. Model $199 organization price, 0.75 support hour and $20 infrastructure: $146.78 contribution/month. Group at $49, 0.3 support hour and $8 infrastructure contributes only $29.03. Acquisition channels are organization communities or membership-system partners, with budget/procurement effort included; no guaranteed cheap distribution. Group retention may follow leadership turnover; association retention can be steadier but slow to win. Development moderate for simple groups, high if promising native integration with several association systems.

**Failure/defense:** too little budget, members ignore tool, annual leadership changes, native features suffice, reports become vanity metrics. Possible defense through one specific institutional workflow and distribution integration. Basic small-group SaaS ranks lower than the handoff product because current substitutes are unusually close and inexpensive.

### 5. Multi-location relationship continuity and routing

**User/payer/trigger:** headquarters or regional operations pays; branch managers, account teams and partnership staff use it when partner relationships lose owners during staff changes, locations duplicate work, or handoffs are stranded across organizational units. Internal continuity can provide value even if outside partners never log in.

**Workflow:** a regional manager leaves. Headquarters sees each affected partner, open commitment, authorized successor and unresolved handoff. The successor accepts responsibility and receives a concise source-linked transition packet. A branch unable to serve a customer offers an authorized alternative after checking capacity and customer permission. Headquarters monitors unowned relationships rather than taking over every conversation.

**Standalone/combined:** staff use it for repeated owner changes and governance, independent of our network. A finite transition service can configure responsibilities; ongoing software remains justified only if ownership changes and coordination recur. A branded multi-city wheel is not required.

**Substitutes/switch:** enterprise CRM, PRM, ticketing and multi-org referral systems already serve substantial portions [E1/E3/E4](04_EVIDENCE_AND_RESEARCH.md). Switching rests on explicit transfer acceptance and partner continuity across the customer's actual systems, not location dashboards alone.

**Data/integrations/participation:** organizational hierarchy, identity/roles, partner scope, owners, open handoffs and audit history. Likely identity/SSO and one or more CRM integrations; HR-triggered transfer is optional and increases privacy scope. Headquarters must assign authority; branches must accept ownership. Tenant separation is essential if separately owned franchisees are involved.

**Automation/humans:** detect missing owners, draft packets and enforce approval steps. Human leaders assign authority and restore trust; automation cannot declare a relationship successfully transferred because a checklist closed.

**Economics:** hypothesized $500–2,000/month/org plus separately scoped onboarding and location/volume bands; example $1,000, three support hours and $100 infrastructure yields $765 contribution. Hypothetical acquisition/onboarding $6,000 gives 7.8-month contribution payback before fixed costs. Long procurement, customization and security demands can invalidate this. Retention may follow organizational use rather than referral seasonality; losing headquarters loses all its locations. Development high: permissions, integration, migration and auditability dominate screen-building effort.

**Failure/defense:** enterprise projects masquerade as SaaS; staff records do not match reality; incumbents cover needs; low location autonomy. Potential defense is durable integration and a specific continuity workflow; it is not evidence of a moat today. Worth an independent research branch for higher contract value, with significantly greater startup downside.

### 6. Human-reviewed trigger and partner-opportunity assistant

**User/payer/trigger:** account managers or relationship operators pay when reviewing permitted customer context for relevant partner needs takes material time. Buyers must already have suitable partners. This is a discovery-assistance product, not an automated lead marketplace.

**Workflow:** a user selects a note stating a customer plans a new office. The assistant identifies possible IT setup and signage needs, cites the sentence, checks partner scope and capacity, and drafts two qualifying questions. The user rejects irrelevant suggestions, confirms the need and decides whether to request introduction permission. No suggestion automatically contacts the customer.

**Standalone/combined:** can improve a firm's existing account reviews without our service. In a managed portfolio it helps preparation; human review remains included labor. It should be useful as a focused assistant rather than a compulsory module in a large platform.

**Substitutes/switch:** CRM AI, general AI plus saved templates, Crossbeam account mapping [E5](04_EVIDENCE_AND_RESEARCH.md). Switch only for reliable context-to-action fit, relevant partner exclusions and auditable suggestions in a repeated workflow. Account overlap is different from evidence of customer need.

**Data/integrations/participation:** user-selected notes, approved partner catalog and trigger examples; optional CRM record access. Whole-inbox reading adds Google verification/security requirements [E9](04_EVIDENCE_AND_RESEARCH.md) and is not assumed cheap. Notes must be sufficiently detailed and current. Permission to hold a note is not automatically permission to share it across companies or model-training datasets.

**Automation/humans:** draft extraction and candidate matching; humans confirm meaning, confidentiality, timeliness, consent and trust. Abstain on weak evidence. Matching must explain both fit and exclusions; no opaque ranking by commission value.

**Economics:** $39–99/user or $99–199/team/month with usage limits; $99 team example, $20 inference/infrastructure and 0.4 support hour produces $62.03 contribution. Hypothetical $800 acquisition/onboarding gives 12.9-month payback before product costs. Retention requires recurring useful accepted suggestions, not generated suggestion count. Development moderate for selected input, high for broad integrations and defensible evaluation. Token cost is only one component; error review and support may dominate.

**Failure/defense:** generic output, false positives, users stop providing notes, disallowed data use, or CRM AI is good enough. Domain exclusions and a permissioned correction/evaluation corpus might improve quality; noisy self-reported outcomes do not establish a data advantage. Conditional specialist option, weaker as a broad AI-first pitch.

### 7. Referral agreement and fee-reconciliation ledger

**User/payer/trigger:** operator or company with already permissible compensated referrals pays when corrections, partial receipts and different agreements make spreadsheets expensive. Finance users approve calculations; software does not offer legal clearance or adjudication.

**Workflow:** a fee agreement specifies eligible first-contract receipts and a cap. A recorded $4,000 eligible receipt yields the agreed fee; a later refund creates a correction linked to the original job. A second claimed source is flagged for approval, not automatically double-paid. A statement is exported for review and external payment.

**Standalone/combined:** useful to existing programs without our service. An operator can pair it with reconciliation labor, priced separately. Does not require a wheel or a marketplace; no handling of customer funds or payouts is included in the base concept.

**Substitutes/switch:** spreadsheets, accounting rules, PartnerStack and referral platforms [E2/E4](04_EVIDENCE_AND_RESEARCH.md). Switching requires reconciliation complexity that those alternatives do not economically handle, such as agreed relationship schedules, partial cash and transparent correction chains. Basic percentage arithmetic is not a product moat.

**Data/integrations/participation:** approved agreement versions, eligible job/referral ID, cash/refund records, payer/payee IDs and decision history. CSV receipt import can suffice conceptually; accounting/billing integration lowers re-entry but adds permission and reconciliation work. Finance staff must certify eligibility. No automated inference of fee rights from every transaction between two firms.

**Automation/humans:** deterministic approved rules, caps, credits and statement calculations; human approval for ambiguity, legal scope, attribution conflict and corrections. AI may extract draft terms but must never silently activate them.

**Economics:** $99–399/month, modeled $199; $25 infrastructure and one support hour yields $133.03 contribution. Hypothetical $1,500 acquisition/onboarding gives 11.3-month payback. Integration/support and liability expectations are high relative to subscription size. Retention from repeated reconciliation; no eligible fee activity means little value. Development high for trustworthy ledger behavior, reversals and version history, even if UI is small.

**Failure/defense:** ineligible or unreported receipts, disputes, low commission volume, buyer expecting legal guarantees or payment custody. Potential defense is trustworthy records and integration in a narrow permitted program. Do not support broad five/ten-year royalties merely to make fees recurring. This is a specialist branch, not the default software foundation.

### 8. Capacity and fit exchange software for an existing provider network

**User/payer/trigger:** network administrator, anchor or established provider consortium pays for failed transfers due to unavailable providers. Providers update availability; request originators use matching. Selling to an existing network avoids making software adoption depend on simultaneously creating marketplace supply.

**Workflow:** an approved source has a customer request it cannot serve. It selects category, service area, timing and consented minimum details. The tool shows qualified providers with recently confirmed availability; expired capacity is marked unknown. A recipient accepts, source receives a receipt, and customer chooses whether to continue.

**Standalone/combined:** an existing network can operate without our managed service. Human network coordination adds vetting, disputes and backup sourcing. A public two-sided marketplace would be a materially more demanding combined business, not a simple SaaS price tier.

**Substitutes/switch:** group messages, preferred-vendor lists, dispatch/field-service systems and internal coordinators. These are conceptual substitutes; no unsupported vendor-specific price comparison. A switch requires fresh capacity, permissioned routing and reliable acceptance with less chasing. Matching from an old directory is worse than an experienced coordinator's current knowledge.

**Data/integrations/participation:** verified scope/service area, availability timestamp/expiry, capacity limits and handoff ownership. Manual refresh is possible; scheduling integration cannot be assumed to represent actual capacity. Provider participation is a core dependency. Do not share competitors' detailed price/margin data or coordinate prices/market allocation [E13](04_EVIDENCE_AND_RESEARCH.md).

**Automation/humans:** filter fit, expire availability and sequence approved notifications. Humans judge service quality, urgency, qualifications and whether a proposed transfer is appropriate; no software certification of competence.

**Economics:** $199–799/month/network or anchor, modeled $299; $35 infrastructure and 1.5 support hours yields $202.53 contribution. Hypothetical $1,800 acquisition/onboarding gives 8.9-month payback. Retention depends on useful repeat overflow. Development moderate–high; dependable real-time integrations can make it much higher. Geography matters for local physical work, not for remote professional-service capacity.

**Failure/defense:** everyone is busy at once, expired data, wrong referrals or networks too small. Defense could be high-quality participating supply in a service area, but that belongs partly to the network operator rather than the software vendor. The vendor's defensibility would need reusable workflow/integration excellence.

## Economic and development comparison

All unit contributions above subtract 3% processing, stated variable technology and replacement-priced support at $35/hour. They do **not** subtract fixed development, maintenance, general overhead, taxes, acquisition or founder compensation beyond support. They are not profit forecasts. Acquisition/onboarding examples include an invented cash-plus-labor allocation; they are not observed channel performance.

| Concept | Illustrative initial product effort / nonlabor cash reserve | Continuing product effort/month, excluding customer support | Main development cost driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-business workspace | 250–500 hours / $2k–8k | 20–40 hours | Usable import/workflow, permissions and messaging |
| Handoff receipt tool | 400–800 / $5k–15k | 30–60 | External access, identity, reliable event/correction flow |
| Operator workbench | 500–900 / $5k–20k | 30–70 | Client isolation and across-client workflow |
| Group/association follow-through | 350–700 / $3k–12k | 20–50 | Roster/roles, private reporting; native suite integrations extra |
| Multi-location continuity | 800–1,600 / $10k–40k | 50–100 | Hierarchy, SSO, migration, procurement and integrations |
| Selected-input AI assistant | 300–700 / $3k–15k | 30–70 | Quality evaluation, exclusions, permissions; broad inbox access extra |
| Fee ledger | 500–1,000 / $5k–20k | 30–70 | Rules, versioning, reversals and accounting reconciliation |
| Existing-network capacity tool | 500–1,000 / $5k–20k | 30–70 | Freshness, routing, fit, event reliability |

These are explicitly invented order-of-magnitude scope allowances, not engineering estimates, vendor quotes or a build plan. At the shared $50/hour founder opportunity cost, 400–800 hours means $20k–40k of founder time before the stated nonlabor reserve. Outsourcing changes actual cash exposure. Complex security reviews, regulated data, broad integration matrices or mobile apps are not included; scope may multiply. A tool assembled quickly still has maintenance, support and accountability costs.

## Standalone, assisted and licensed forms compared fairly

| Form | Customer buys | Attractive condition | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standalone SaaS | Tool; customer operates relationships | Customer has motivation, owners and flow | Low data participation, CRM substitution, software support exceeds price |
| SaaS with finite onboarding | Tool plus a defined migration/configuration job | Repeated configuration is reusable and separately funded | Every onboarding becomes custom consulting |
| Software-enabled managed service | Operator outcomes using software | Customer wants work removed and pays for both labor and infrastructure | Calling bundled receipts SaaS hides labor and churn exposure |
| Software plus optional exception desk | Tool plus capped work only when wanted | Routine flow works and exceptions have a bounded cost | Heavy users consume unlimited service under subscription |
| White-label/reseller license | Existing operator distributes branded instances | Partner already serves buyers and owns first-line support | Discount and reseller concentration erase margin; support falls back on vendor |
| Branded operator network/license | Brand, playbooks, quality support and software | Founder wants training and governance responsibilities | Adds brand/quality and possible franchise questions; not just a software license |

Invented reseller example: direct price $149, 30% reseller share leaves $104.30 before modeled 3% processing ($3.13), $15 technology and 0.25 hour second-line support ($8.75): $77.42 contribution. This can be attractive if distribution savings exceed the $34.61 difference from the $112.03 direct case. If second-line support becomes 1.5 hours, contribution falls to $33.67. One reseller with 50 end customers is one commercial concentration, not 50 independent acquisition successes. White-label terms, data export, support ownership and brand control must be explicit in conceptual economics.

## What to preserve from the original blueprint

Preserve partner scope, triggers, referrals, named commitments, limited outcomes and role permissions as a vocabulary. Chapters, memberships, goals, broad KPI snapshots, experiments, campaigns and meeting libraries are optional to the specific buyer job. A single-company tool need not have chapters. A fee ledger needs agreement versions and corrections more than a wheel diagram. A continuity tool needs owner transfer acceptance more than growth scorecards.

The former paid-service-pilot prerequisite is superseded. The appropriate question here is **which independently useful software product has the strongest hypothetical buyer economics and least costly adoption requirements?** The current answer is conditional: handoff receipts first for a focused small-business workflow; continuity for a more ambitious organizational product; operator workbench for serving other connectors. All three require deeper substitute analysis before any confident market conclusion.
