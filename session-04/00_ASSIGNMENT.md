This entire project is an evaluation and creation of business options. Its deliverable is a well-developed set of possibilities that helps me understand what I could build and operate.

All testing in this project means hypothetical situations, simulations, scenario analysis and economic stress tests. Do not steer toward real-world pilots, outreach, interviews, deposits, customer recruitment or launch preparation. Do not make those activities the next step.

I have not selected a business model. Session 3 rankings are provisional analyst opinions. The partnership-transition service is one candidate, not the chosen direction.

Explore software now, alongside the other business options. Do not require a service business to be operated or validated before considering software as a standalone opportunity.

## 1. Reconstruct the full option space

Organize the existing concepts into understandable business families, including:

- Small managed referral wheels and ongoing relationship coordination.
- One-buyer partner management.
- Finite partnership activation, transition and process-installation services.
- Anchor-funded, association-funded and event-based networks.
- Software businesses.
- Software combined with services or networks.
- Additional concepts that materially improve on the existing alternatives.

Separate the business itself from its payment structure, distribution channel and expansion model. Avoid presenting pricing variants as entirely different businesses.

Do not narrow immediately to the previous top three. Explain which concepts overlap, which are distinct and which deserve further development.

## 2. Explore software as a full business branch

Consider software for individual businesses, independent relationship operators, existing referral groups, chambers/associations and multi-location organizations.

Examine possibilities such as partner management, referral handoffs, trigger recognition, matching, accountability, attribution, fee administration and useful AI assistance. Invent better alternatives where appropriate.

For each credible software concept explain:

- Who uses it, who pays and what problem prompts purchase.
- A concrete example of the user workflow.
- Its value without an accompanying managed service.
- Its value when paired with a service or network.
- Existing substitutes and why someone might switch.
- Required data, integrations and participation.
- What software can automate and what still depends on human trust or judgment.
- Plausible pricing, acquisition, support, retention and development economics.
- Principal failure modes and potential defensibility.

Explore standalone SaaS, software-enabled services and licensing/white-label options fairly. “Explore now” does not mean “build now”; conceptual product design and hypothetical economics are sufficient.

## 3. Give me a clear plus/minus comparison

Use a readable overview matrix and fuller explanations for materially distinct options.

Compare buyer value, participating-business profitability, my daily role, recurring versus finite revenue, acquisition difficulty, operating workload, startup effort, software requirements, scalability, founder dependence, commercial/legal constraints and downside risk.

Honor my interest in being a connector and ongoing relationship operator. Vallejo and surrounding cities matter for local concepts, but do not impose local geography on software or other models without a reason.

Show the strongest argument for and against each option. Explain what assumptions would make it attractive and what would make it unattractive.

## 4. Play the options through hypothetical situations

Use consistent assumptions where comparison is meaningful. Clearly label invented figures.

Include situations such as:

- Slow adoption or little referral activity.
- One participant generating most of the value.
- Unequal deal sizes and recipient margins.
- Partners communicating directly.
- Relationships becoming self-sufficient.
- Founder delegation.
- Strong demand creating excessive work.
- Customer churn or loss of an anchor buyer.
- Software users failing to enter data.
- Existing CRM tools providing an adequate substitute.
- Automation lowering workload but increasing errors or support.
- Expansion to more customers, operators or territories.

Show the mechanism, financial implications and possible redesign—not just a risk label. A hypothetical success is not evidence of actual demand.

## 5. Identify what deserves further research

Use existing research first, then investigate gaps that could materially change the comparison. Cite current sources for external claims and separate evidence, founder reports, inference and assumptions.

Do not default to “run a pilot” when evidence is unavailable. Explain what remains unknowable through desk research and how that uncertainty affects the options.

Recommendations are welcome, but make them conditional. Do not equate the easiest option to test with the best business for me.
