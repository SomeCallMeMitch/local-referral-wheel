# Session 4 — Evidence, limitations and research that could change the comparison

Research checked 2026-09-13. This is public desk research, not customer research or legal clearance. Provider descriptions establish advertised offers; they do not establish product quality, adoption, realized ROI, market size or willingness to switch. Prices below are displayed USD prices, not negotiated quotes, and can change. No accounts, trials or demonstrations requiring registration were created.

## Evidence types

- **Founder report:** two complementary e-commerce service companies generated several deals and paid the founder; activity weakened after delegation. Counts, margins, causal incrementality, time and collections are unaudited. See [original reconstruction](../session-03/01_ORIGINAL_MODEL_RECONSTRUCTION.md).
- **Founder preference:** connector and ongoing relationship operator; small connected groups; recipient profitability; Vallejo and surrounding cities relevant locally; software and several alternatives remain open. No fixed income, capital, fee or time target supplied.
- **External evidence:** sources below and qualified prior public research. Vendor outcome claims are not independent evidence.
- **Inference:** a buyer/job or differentiation argument derived from the above. It may be wrong.
- **Assumption:** every Session 4 forecast, price proposal, workload, conversion, churn, acquisition cost and financial stress case. None is market measurement.

## What useful prior work survives

[Session 3 local research](../session-03/07_LOCAL_MARKET_REALITY_CHECK.md) identified overlapping candidate records around Vallejo/Benicia: roughly nine B2B, eight home-service, and nine property/vendor records. These are historical screening counts from the same date, with overlap; this session did not recount them. They are neither market size nor willing customers. BizNet was rechecked; current BNI local rosters and dues remain unknown.

[Fee-boundary research](../session-03/08_CALIFORNIA_COMPENSATION_BOUNDARIES.md) retains sector-specific primary links for insurance, real estate, legal, healthcare, investment and post-exit questions. Those sectors are not independently cleared or comprehensively re-researched here. Keep them out of generic transaction-fee assumptions. Software that calculates a fee does not make that fee permissible; ordinary administrative subscriptions avoid some outcome-compensation complexity but are not universal safe harbors.

The original [software blueprint](../06_SAAS_PRODUCT_BLUEPRINT.md) remains a useful domain inventory. Its business-KPI and intervention/outcome dataset is observational and selected: improvements may reflect seasonality, stronger participants or unrelated changes. A statistical recommendation advantage would require comparable definitions, permissions, sufficient data and a credible causal design. Merely accumulating records is not defensibility.

## Highest-value remaining desk research

| Priority / uncertainty | Bounded research output | What could reverse the comparison |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Cross-company handoff advantage | Compare public workflow documentation for referral tools and CRMs: external status update, privacy, no paid external seat, CRM coexistence, corrections, exports | If an incumbent already solves the entire chosen workflow adequately and cheaply, favor an integration/installation service or another software job |
| 2. Founder economics | Recalculate all options at founder-selected monthly income, hours, capital exposure and desired relationship time | High relationship preference lifts stewardship; very low support tolerance weakens SaaS; low capital favors services without declaring them best |
| 3. Narrow software segment | Public vertical workflows, integration permissions, API limits, data-handling requirements and competitive feature/price comparison | A repeated, high-value handoff task with one dominant CRM strengthens a narrow product; fragmented tools and sparse events weaken it |
| 4. Payer economics | Public financial statements where available, transparent service cost examples and bounded margin scenarios; do not infer private job profit from trade averages | Thin recipient surplus shifts payment toward anchor/customer or removes manual coordination entirely |
| 5. Independent operators and associations | Public directories, association budgets/annual reports and existing software contracts where public; deduplicate organizations | Small addressable operator population or low budget makes software for individual businesses comparatively stronger. Public budgets still do not reveal willingness to spend on us |
| 6. Local architecture | Revisit directory category depth, actual published service areas, licensing records and existing networking programs | Deep replacement supply supports wheels; limited overlap favors a buyer portfolio, a supplier ecosystem or nonlocal B2B service |
| 7. Legal and commercial forms | Compare public regulator guidance against precisely described hypothetical activity/payment/data flows | A prohibited activity can eliminate a configuration; a retainer label cannot rescue it. White-label software terms must be distinguished from brand/territory/operator-control arrangements that warrant separate franchise analysis |
| 8. Delegation and continuity | Synthesize publicly documented alliance governance and employee-transition cases; contrast authority, incentives, client knowledge and demand exhaustion explanations | Transferable workflow evidence can strengthen either transition services or continuity software; it cannot prove transfer for this founder |

## What desk research cannot settle

Actual willingness to pay our price, referral frequency among unknown partners, private margins, willingness to report, renewal, interpersonal trust, acquisition cost, staff handover success and the founder's enjoyment cannot be established by public comparisons or favorable simulations. Even audited results elsewhere would not settle this particular business. Keep these as unresolved variables, use downside ranges, and present conditional options. There is no instruction here to remove uncertainty through interviews, recruitment or a pilot.

The appropriate stopping point is an informed portfolio with visible uncertainty. Future sessions can deepen conceptual products, substitute analysis and simulated economics without becoming launch preparation.
