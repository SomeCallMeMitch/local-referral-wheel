# Session 4 — The business options, separated clearly

2026-09-13. Evaluation only. All commercial designs and numerical examples are hypotheses. No business selected. Read this overview, then [software concepts](02_SOFTWARE_BUSINESSES.md), [hypothetical economics](03_HYPOTHETICAL_ECONOMICS_AND_SCENARIOS.md), and [evidence/research](04_EVIDENCE_AND_RESEARCH.md).

The strongest conclusion is a separation, not a winner: **creating relationships, operating relationships, processing handoffs, and selling workflow software are different customer jobs.** They can coexist, but none is a prerequisite for considering another. A finite transition project may be a good business while being a poor match for a founder who most enjoys ongoing connecting. Software can be useful to customers who already operate their own relationships without this founder ever running their network.

## Four layers that must not be confused

1. **Business:** what work/result is bought and by whom—e.g., running a mutual partner network or providing an external handoff tool.
2. **Payment:** dues, retainer, fixed project, subscription, usage band, capped success fee, creditable minimum. Dues-only and success-only wheels are versions of the same network business with different risk allocation.
3. **Distribution:** direct business sales, independent operators, a chamber, a software ecosystem or a supplier. A chamber can distribute a tool without buying managed operations.
4. **Expansion:** additional clients, small wheels, locations, coordinators, resellers or territories. More cities is not a new value proposition. White-label software is a licensing/distribution form; a branded operating network adds a distinct quality-control obligation.

The original broad growth council combined network, advice, implementation and dashboards. Multiple benefits can improve retention only if customers value them and delivery cost fits. Bundling is not evidence of that complementarity. Preserve the components as options rather than quietly rebuilding the bundle.

## When the service and network options become attractive or unattractive

**Mutual relationship stewardship.** Attractive when several complementary firms repeatedly see relevant customer needs, sources gain real customer-care or reciprocal benefit, and continued coordination earns its cost. It best honors the founder's interest in maintaining a small network. Unattractive when the founder must continually persuade disengaged firms or bills relationships that run themselves. Direct contact is desirable; continued trigger discovery, replacement and difficult handoffs must justify renewal. Member-level profitability, not aggregate referred revenue, is the test in the simulation.

**One-buyer portfolio management.** Attractive when one firm values several active partnerships and has ongoing changes in clients, capacity and partners. Clear accountability does not require equal referrals. The founder works for the buyer, so recommending another provider when the buyer is full can create a conflict that needs explicit scope. Unattractive if the buyer expects unlimited prospecting, sales closing or work that staff could do cheaply. Remote B2B portfolios need not be Vallejo-based; local service partners need real service-area overlap.

**Handoff operations / introduction desk.** Recovery has the best task clarity when flow already exists, but follow-up is not automatically a large business. A pure introduction desk instead needs continual qualified source supply, which must be costed separately. Both become unattractive at low ticket value or when recontacting people is the only service. Bounded exception work can complement software; internal automation that resolves everything removes the human retainer's rationale.

**Finite activation, transition and installation.** Activation develops a new/dormant pairing; transition moves authority and account knowledge to new owners; installation configures a workflow that an empowered team already wants. Transition has the strongest historical connection, but a single delegation failure does not prove a widespread purchasing category. Attractive for organizations wanting independence and for a founder comfortable finishing and selling the next project. Unattractive if the founder primarily wants enduring relationships, or if the apparent deliverable lacks authority to change staff incentives. A successful hypothetical handover is completed value, not evidence of demand.

**Anchor/supplier ecosystem.** Attractive when a supplier or business with repeat customer problems benefits from appropriate supporting providers, even without being the recipient of every job. A supplier's customer adoption/retention can be a different value pool from vendor referral fees. This is the strongest network candidate for funding asymmetric participation. It is unattractive when the anchor wants exclusive steering, broad emergency coverage or a subsidy disguised as neutral recommendations. Property settings add sector complexity; the concept also applies to ordinary B2B suppliers without local geography.

**Association operations.** Attractive when an existing organization wants to purchase actual follow-through and has both budget and an empowered staff sponsor. It saves building an entirely new membership, not the work of activating members. Unattractive if the institution purchases reports while members ignore handoffs. ChamberMaster already records referrals [E8](04_EVIDENCE_AND_RESEARCH.md); the service must solve follow-through or staffing beyond available administration.

**Event coordination.** Attractive where a recurring stream of openings, turnovers or other events has valuable sequencing needs. Customer-paid concierge reduces provider-fee incentive conflicts; anchor purchase reduces repeated customer acquisition. These are funding variants until service obligations change. Unattractive if customers want a general contractor, mover or property manager rather than a connector. Coordination must not silently include licensed negotiation, construction management or full project responsibility.

**Overflow exchange.** Attractive where quality providers regularly decline suitable jobs, want to protect customer goodwill and can identify trusted backups. It can work without forced two-way referrals. Unattractive when availability expires faster than updates, best providers are all busy together, or participants expect emergency response for a small subscription. This is a distinct problem worth developing, not an automatic marketplace opportunity.

**Partner campaigns.** Attractive for a founder who enjoys marketing production and can bound approvals and scope. Unattractive if campaign writing displaces the preferred relationship role. Existing agency substitutes can be adequate. No raw audience-list exchange is necessary for the hypothetical concept: each partner can communicate with its own permitted audience.

**Council and implementation club.** Preserve because they solve different problems without referral flow. The council sells judgment and trusted peers; the implementation club sells completed work. Neither is a priority on currently stated role preference. They rise only if the founder actually wants coaching/facilitation or production. A broad bundle earns consideration only when combined value exceeds combined costs; the earlier original package did not establish this.

## Recipient profitability across all families

For any participant, benefit must exceed incremental delivery/selling costs, participation time, fees, uncertainty reserve and the opportunity cost of taking this work instead of something else. Existing referred revenue is not automatically incremental. A source can rationally contribute without receiving the same number of leads if it gains customer retention, solved client problems or reliable overflow; that benefit cannot simply be assumed or assigned an invented dollar value to make the model work.

In anchor and association models, track payer economics separately from vendor/member economics. In software, saved employee time is value only if it frees useful capacity or cost; it need not become immediate payroll savings. SaaS fees passed through by an operator still reduce the recipient's budget. No configuration earns a pass just because the founder's margin looks attractive.

## New refinements that improve the available alternatives

- **Handoff receipt and exception tool:** a narrowly shared record showing acceptance, owner, next action and closure while each firm retains its CRM. It addresses cross-company coordination without requiring a new network or full database migration.
- **Relationship continuity system:** named executive sponsor, operating owner, transition packet and overdue ownership transfer, especially across locations. It is useful beyond one finite founder handover if staff changes and partner changes recur.
- **Operator software with separately metered exception help:** external operators can buy software alone; those wanting help purchase a capped service. Product revenue and human labor remain visible. It does not require owning their brand or territories.

These are original product-design inferences, not uniqueness claims. They improve scope and incentive design, but the source register shows substantial competing functionality. Further development should compare them against incumbents, not assume an empty market.
