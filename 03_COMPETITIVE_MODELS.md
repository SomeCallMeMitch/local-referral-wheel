# 03 --- Competitive and Adjacent Models

## FRAP Chapters

FRAP demonstrates demand for a simpler, lower-market peer-growth model. Its public pitch combines a business-growth framework with local chapter leadership and recurring member fees. The relevant lesson is not to copy its proprietary materials; it is that someone is actively commercializing a chapter-based growth/advisory product for smaller business owners.

Useful lesson:
- simple frameworks sell;
- chapter leaders can monetize facilitation;
- recurring member dues are central;
- curriculum/structure reduce facilitator burden;
- a packaged operating system can later become a licensable product.

Source: https://frapchapters.com/intro1

## Vistage

Vistage is powerful evidence that owners/executives pay for facilitated peer advisory. Public Vistage materials describe:
- 12--16 noncompeting executives in a group;
- monthly meetings;
- professional Chair facilitation;
- confidentiality;
- structured issue processing;
- one-to-one coaching;
- expert speakers;
- accountability and progress tracking.

What to borrow at the principle level:
- careful member selection;
- noncompetition;
- confidentiality;
- facilitation matters;
- recurring cadence;
- accountability;
- combine group wisdom with structured process.

Do not copy Vistage's proprietary issue-processing method.

Sources:
- https://vistage.com/membership/our-approach/
- https://pages.vistage.com/membership-info
- https://vistage.com/membership/member-experience-one-to-one-coaching/

## The Alternative Board (TAB)

TAB's public materials describe:
- 8--10 local, noncompeting business-owner peers;
- monthly half-day facilitated advisory meetings;
- one-to-one coaching;
- confidentiality;
- issue preparation;
- monthly billing;
- long member tenure (TAB publicly states an average tenure of 4.7 years);
- one public TAB article cites typical membership dues of roughly $600--$900/month, although local pricing varies.

This is especially relevant because its target is closer to privately held SMB owners than high-end CEO groups.

Sources:
- https://www.thealternativeboard.com/become-a-member
- https://www.thealternativeboard.com/advisory-boards-and-mastermind-groups
- https://www.thealternativeboard.com/blog/guide-to-finding-right-peer-advisory-group

## BNI

BNI validates a different part of the model: structured referral behavior and category exclusivity.

Public BNI materials describe:
- one professional per specialty in a chapter;
- weekly structured meetings;
- learning members' businesses deeply;
- repeated interactions that build enough trust for referrals;
- passing and receiving referrals.

Important lesson: **category exclusivity is not merely scarcity marketing. It reduces internal competition and increases willingness to share.**

Sources:
- https://www.bni.com/the-latest/blog-news/what-is-bni-and-how-it-works/
- https://www.bni.com/the-latest/blog-news/why-bni-allows-only-one-profession-per-chapter/

## Competitive whitespace

The proposed model can occupy space between networking and high-end advisory:

**Networking groups:** strong referral emphasis, weaker implementation/advisory.

**Peer advisory:** strong decision support, typically more expensive/time intensive, referrals are secondary.

**Business coaching:** personalized guidance, but network effects are weaker.

**Marketing agency:** implementation, but usually narrow service scope and no peer network.

**Proposed system:** referrals + advisory + implementation + measurable dashboard.

## Differentiation principles

1. **Measure value.** Do not ask members to "feel" that the group is worthwhile.
2. **Implement, don't merely teach.**
3. **Use trigger-based referrals rather than generic "anyone who needs X."**
4. **Create category exclusivity carefully.**
5. **Make every meeting produce commitments/assets/experiments.**
6. **Use software to preserve institutional memory.**
7. **Keep meeting burden lower than traditional full-day peer boards unless the market asks for more.**
8. **Design for SMB owners who are established enough to pay but underserved by premium CEO networks.**

## Competitive danger

Trying to be BNI + Vistage + agency + SaaS simultaneously can produce a confusing offer. The launch version needs one clear promise. A strong candidate:

> A private group of noncompeting local owners that helps you create more revenue from referrals, existing customers, and practical growth improvements --- with every result tracked.

The components can be richer behind the scenes than the initial pitch.
