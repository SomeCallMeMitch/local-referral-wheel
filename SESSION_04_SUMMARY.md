# Session 4 summary — business options, including standalone software

Completed 2026-09-13. Repository: SomeCallMeMitch/local-referral-wheel. **No model selected. No implementation or real-world testing is within this project's scope.** Testing means hypothetical scenarios, simulations and economic stress tests. Prior rankings are analyst opinions, not founder decisions. This summary and CONTINUE_HERE.md govern over conflicting historical instructions.

## 1. The distinct choices and their principal pluses/minuses

| Choice | Principal plus | Principal minus |
|---|---|---|
| Small managed referral wheel / mutual relationship stewardship | Best fit for ongoing mutual connecting and maintaining a small network | Several businesses must keep benefiting; low flow, unequal giving and billing friction can unravel it |
| One-buyer partner portfolio | Clear payer and ongoing relationship work | Buyer concentration and scope creep; mature relationships may need less help |
| Handoff/recovery operations | Observable work on existing flow; easier to bound than general business development | Can become low-value chasing; a CRM or staff procedure may suffice |
| Finite activation, transition or installation | Sell a bounded result; self-sufficiency is successful completion | Continuous project sales and less enduring relationship work |
| Anchor/supplier-funded ecosystem | A buyer funds coverage/customer success without equal referrals | Anchor can dominate revenue and routing; availability expectations raise workload |
| Association-funded operations | Work with an existing membership and institutional buyer | Budget, governance and actual member participation are different hurdles |
| Event coordination / customer concierge | Specific customer need; new events replenish opportunities | Urgency, repeated acquisition and project-management scope creep |
| Capacity/overflow exchange | Helps sources preserve goodwill and recipients accept suitable work | Availability and quality must be current; both sides may be busy simultaneously |
| Partner campaign service | Concrete joint marketing deliverable | Production and approvals can turn the founder into an agency operator |
| Owner council / implementation club | Valuable decision/execution job independent of referrals | Coaching/facilitation/production is less aligned with the stated connector role |
| Standalone software | Serve existing businesses or operators without running their networks; wider reach | Acquisition, support, adoption and development exposure; strong substitutes |
| Software plus bounded operations / operator licensing | Combine relationship work with reusable tools, or equip other connectors | Both product and service costs; licensing adds channel dependence |

Dues, retainers, success fees and creditable minimums are payment structures, not additional businesses. Territories, coordinators and locations are expansion paths. An association can be buyer, channel or both; these roles have different economics. No option is mandatory because the repository is named Referral Wheel.

## 2. Strongest possibilities within each branch

**Ongoing service: one-buyer partner portfolio management.** It best combines a clear payer with the founder's interest in creating and sustaining partnerships. It leads conditionally if there is recurring useful work, a buyer with adequate economic benefit and a scope that can transfer to a coordinator. The inherited five-client model leaves only $120/month after an invented coordinator block and founder time at $50/hour, so pricing/workload sensitivity matters. Finite transition/installation remains the strongest project-service alternative for a founder who enjoys completing engagements and selling the next one.

**Network: anchor/supplier-funded ecosystem for funding resilience; small mutual wheel for personal role fit.** An anchor can pay for customer success or reliable access even when vendors do not exchange equal value. It brings concentration and scope risk. A mutual wheel better preserves the ongoing connector role across several firms, but its participant-level economics must survive low activity and uneven source value. Neither is universally better. Local versions should use actual Vallejo/nearby service-area overlap; supplier/B2B ecosystems need not be local.

**Standalone software: cross-company handoff receipts and exception management.** One company pays to know who accepted an introduction and who owns the next action, while counterparties make limited updates and each keeps its CRM. This can have independent value without our service. The key uncertainty is whether that precise workflow is sufficiently better than existing tools. Basic CRM functions are already available, and referral products already include portals, integrations and easier access. [HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm), [Referral Rock](https://referralrock.com/pricing/).

**Higher-complexity software: multi-location relationship continuity.** Preserving ownership through staff/branch changes may support larger organizational contracts and internal utility even without partner logins. Enterprise integration, procurement and customization can overwhelm the economics. The independent-operator workbench is also credible, but the number and budgets of suitable operators remain unresolved.

**Combined: operator workbench with separately priced, bounded onboarding or exception operations.** Customers can use software alone or purchase defined human help. This preserves a relationship-oriented role without pretending human work is free. Supporting other operators may fit differently from personally maintaining local partnerships. Licensing existing software is also a valid combined model; proprietary development must earn its cost. Neither requires operating a service before researching software.

The other software concepts—one-business partner workspace, group/association follow-through, human-reviewed trigger assistant, fee-reconciliation ledger and existing-network capacity tool—remain developed alternatives. Generic small-group software is weaker because close substitutes are inexpensive, including a free small-group tier from [Referral Grove](https://referralgrove.com/pricing) and lower-priced group plans from [Referral Hopper](https://referralhopper.com/pricing/). Provider pages are evidence of advertised offers, not independent product performance.

## 3. What holds up and changes from earlier rankings

Holds up: recipient profitability comes before fee maximization; attribution is not incremental profit; trust and authority do not transfer automatically; broad long-term royalties are an unreliable foundation; existing tools are serious substitutes; small groups can expand through coordinators without becoming giant chapters.

Changes: transition is no longer the default first choice; ongoing-role fit and repeat selling alter its appeal. Software is a current independent branch, not a later reward for running a service. Anchor funding receives more weight for handling asymmetry. A dues wheel is not automatically less suitable than a hybrid, and success-only is not a separate business to rank as though its product were different. Small-group SaaS is downgraded on current competitive evidence; narrowly useful handoff and continuity software are upgraded for further conceptual development.

No numerical overall score is assigned: unknown preferences and uncertain economics would make one decimal ranking misleading. The easiest engagement to arrange would not establish the best business to own.

## 5. Preference questions that would most change the comparison

- How much of an ideal week would be personal partner conversations versus designing systems, supporting software users and managing staff?
- What monthly owner income would make a business worthwhile, and how long could it take to reach it?
- How much cash and unpaid development/selling time would you be comfortable risking on an uncertain option?
- Do you prefer serving a few larger companies, maintaining a mutual local network, or serving many software customers—including customers outside this area?
- Would you enjoy selling and completing finite projects, or is continuity with the same partners a major source of value to you?
- Would you rather operate partnerships yourself or help other independent connectors operate theirs?
- How much urgent follow-up, customer support and employee management would you accept?
- Is local community identity an important part of the desired business, or mainly a convenient starting context?

Unanswered preferences are not grounds to choose on the founder's behalf. Public research cannot establish actual demand, trust, reporting behavior, customer margins or renewal. The analysis retains those uncertainties rather than making real-world testing the next step.
