# Customer discovery before selling
Goal: understand actual behavior and choose among A, E and C. No outreach has been conducted. Interview 12 qualified owners across likely sources/recipients; include at least four outside close friends to reduce politeness bias. Ask permission to take notes. Do not collect customer-identifying data unnecessarily.

## Sixteen questions
1. Walk me through the last new customer who came from another business.
2. How did the introduction happen, and what happened next?
3. What made that customer a good or bad fit?
4. What did you do the last time a referral went unanswered or badly?
5. Which businesses have you recommended in the last 60 days, and why?
6. Which customer needs do you notice but struggle to refer confidently?
7. What proportion of new work could you actually accept right now, and which kind?
8. What partner relationships take effort to maintain? What happens if you stop?
9. Tell me about a network or group you paid for. What did you pay, use and renew or cancel?
10. What other spending or time competes with partnership development?
11. Who helps you make difficult business decisions today? Describe a recent example.
12. What growth improvement have you postponed? What prevented implementation?
13. What meeting schedule have you actually maintained over the last six months?
14. What referral information would you comfortably share privately? What is off limits?
15. Have you paid for a lead, introduction or closed deal? How was the fee determined and disputed?
16. What would make you stop participating in a partner arrangement even if it occasionally sent work?

Probe answers with dates, examples and actions; avoid turning each response into a pitch.

## Separate concept comparison after discovery
Show short descriptions of A (dues circle), E (ongoing relationship coordination with deal-linked fees), and C (implementation). Rotate presentation order across conversations. Ask which, if any, would replace something they already do/pay for and why. Then show full three-month cost examples at zero, modest and high deal volume. Ask what would prevent a real purchase. Keep stated preference separate from paid behavior.

For E ask: "If a relationship you already knew starts generating more work because someone coordinates it, what work would that person need to continue doing to earn an ongoing fee?" Also ask about repeat deals, post-exit fees, record sharing and direct introductions outside the program. Do not assume willingness to sign a fee agreement means willingness to report receipts later.

## Avoid
"Wouldn't better referrals help?"; "Would you pay $199 for ten times the ROI?"; "Do you like our idea?"; hypothetical guaranteed lead volume; combining five benefits until the prospect agrees with one; implying scarcity before seats exist. Do not ask for competitor/customer confidential prices in a group.

## Demand vs politeness
Strong: recent recurring pain, existing spending, asks specific questions about fit/terms, introduces another suitable owner, completes profile, pays under understood terms, attends and updates real referrals, renews.
Weak: compliments, "keep me posted," agreement to a free event, vague willingness to pay, refundable deposit without subsequent payment, referral to someone else because the interviewee has no interest.

## Transition to invitation
Summarize their problem in their words; ask if they want a concrete pilot invitation. If yes, provide the exact scope, price, dates, exclusions and cancellation terms. No hard sell before understanding fit. Discovery subjects are not automatically enrolled or added to marketing lists.

## Interview record
Date, category, maturity, access source, available capacity, recent-event evidence, current substitute/spend, pain, quote with permission, concept preference, objections, permission for follow-up, next concrete action, paid/not paid. Record negative evidence as carefully as positive. Stop/recruit differently if first 12 interviews cannot substantiate at least six real adjacent customer events and four owners with a clear current coordination problem.
