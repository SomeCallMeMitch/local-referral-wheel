# Pricing and unit economics
All figures USD, assumptions dated 2026-09-13. No customer willingness-to-pay evidence. Membership and deal-desk businesses are modeled separately. No software/licensing revenue supports these calculations.

## A: steady-state dues-only circle
Monthly cash costs assumed: $200 fixed (simple tools/venue/admin allowance) + 3% payment processing. This is a modeling allowance, not vendor pricing. Insurance, legal setup, taxes, travel and unusual disputes are not quoted; additional recurring costs lower returns dollar-for-dollar. Founder-time target: $50/hour opportunity cost, awaiting founder preference.

Hours: recruiting/replacement 2; meeting prep 2; meeting 1.25; common follow-up 1; common referral admin 1.25; general support 1; narrow implementation prep/review 1.5 = 10 fixed hours/month. Plus onboarding/relationship maintenance .2/member, referral admin .4/member, individual follow-up .2/member, support .2/member = 1 hour/member. Total H = 10 + N. These are capped baseline estimates, not observed time.

## Acquisition, startup and first-90-day reality
Assume 32 founder hours to recruit the founding cohort and 16 setup hours for profiles/materials/process design, in addition to delivery. Eight-member 90-day delivery requires 54 steady-state hours; total first-cohort work = 102 hours. Three-month revenue = $4,776; modeled recurring cash costs = $743.28; contribution = $4,032.72; first-cohort contribution/hour = $39.54 BEFORE one-time legal/insurance/setup cash costs.

At $50/hour labor value, first-cohort economic contribution = $4,032.72 − $5,100 = **−$1,067.28**, before those unquoted costs. Allow this only as an explicitly budgeted learning investment, not proof the founder is earning $75/hour from launch.

If recruitment takes 64 instead of 32 hours, total 134 hours yields $30.09/hour before one-time cash costs. Do not omit recruiting or count unpaid founder labor as free.

Steady-state base cost allows replacement outreach but not repeated complete recruitment. At 8×$199, labor-adjusted surplus with 18 hours at $50 = $444.24/month. A $600 one-time setup bill alone absorbs about 1.35 months of that surplus. A single vacant seat also reduces economics; use actual member-months in reporting.

## E: success-fee desk scenarios
Fee bases are hypothetical first-job **collected revenue**, excluding tax, refunds and agreed pass-through items. Recipient pays operator. This payer/basis is an assumption pending clarification of the original wheel. No fee on lifetime customer value. Percentages are not assertions of legality or market acceptability.

Hours H = 12 fixed + .5 per qualified intro Q + .5 per collected win W. Fixed hours include source maintenance/routing availability/admin; initial acquisition and setup are extra. Cash costs = $150 fixed + 3% collected fees. Wins below represent a mature month's expected collected results, not immediate cash from that month's introductions.

| Scenario | Q/month | Collected wins W | Job value V | 10% fee revenue | H | Gross/hour | After-cost/hour |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sparse | 8 | 1.6 | $1500 | $240 | 16.8 | $14.29 | $4.93 |
| Base | 20 | 5 | $3000 | $1500 | 24.5 | $61.22 | $53.27 |
| Strong | 32 | 9.6 | $5000 | $4800 | 32.8 | $146.34 | $137.38 |

Fractional wins are expected-value illustrations; actual cash arrives in whole uneven jobs. Scenarios combine different assumed close/collection rates (20%, 25%, 30%) and job values; they are NOT forecasts or independent statistical cases.

**Break-even at $50/hour:** with 25% collected-win conversion, Q=4W, so H=12+2.5W. At $3,000/job and 10%, contribution after processing/fixed costs is 291W−150. To cover labor: 291W−150 >= 600+125W → W >= 4.52 collected wins/month, requiring about 18.1 qualified introductions/month. These rates must be observed, not borrowed from a sales benchmark.

**Low base + fee:** 8 recipients × $99 + 5 wins × ($3,000×5%) = $1,542/month; H=24.5; after-cost contribution $1,345.74 or $54.93/hour. Cap assumed $500/job. This is close to the modeled base 10% desk economics, but changes who carries dry-month risk. $99 dues alone provide $792/month, not proof recipients will stay without deals.

**Collection lag:** if first wins pay in month 3, a no-dues desk could receive no revenue for months 1–2 while funding operations and source work. For the base workload that is $300 cash overhead plus 49 hours, excluding setup, before collection. Hybrid dues fund some availability but also create immediate recurring-service expectations. Model invoice dates and actual cash, not only signed deals.

## What 10% takes from recipient economics
Illustrative $3,000 job: a $300 fee consumes 50% of gross profit at 20% gross margin, 33.3% at 30%, and 20% at 50%. Gross profit is still before relevant overhead and other acquisition/coordination costs. A job may be worth taking when truly incremental, capacity is available and contribution is positive; high ticket alone proves none of these.

Example at 30% margin: $900 gross profit − $300 fee − $100 extra handling = $500 before fixed overhead. If 5 jobs also require $99 monthly dues, allocate $19.80/job for a full cost comparison. Do not compare fee against revenue alone.

## Payment designs to explore
| Design | Illustrative terms | Upside | Main failure / test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dues only | $199/month | Simple predictable funding | Pay despite no deals; test renewal |
| No dues, percentage | 10% eligible first-job cash | Aligns fee with receipts | Margin/lag/leakage; reconstruct real invoices |
| Low dues + capped percentage | $99 + 5%, cap $500/job | Funds availability with outcome participation | Double-charge concern; show total costs |
| Fixed completed-deal fee | $150 per agreed qualified paid job | Less revenue auditing | Bad fit across job sizes; agree categories |
| Share of gross profit | e.g. 15% disclosed job gross profit | Reflects margin differences | Cost definitions/audit disputes; probably too complex initially |
| Fee credit | $99/month credited against month's success fees | Avoid perceived double charge | Revenue is max(base, fees), not their sum; not free extra margin |
| Refundable bond | e.g. $100 returned under clear completion terms | Commitment without permanent dues | Liability, weak demand evidence; not earned revenue |
| Setup project | e.g. $500 for bounded mapping/intros | Honest finite job | Must cover initial labor; not recurring proof |

All fee-bearing structures need arrangement-specific review; a flat fee or credit is not a workaround for prohibited referral compensation.

## Commercial choices
Founding discount: protect price for 90 days, not forever. Increase only after members verify value and accept a new disclosed offer; test one increase at renewal, not surprise billing. If hours exceed plan, reduce scope before assuming price can rise.

No setup fee in A pilot; test separately if onboarding is valuable standalone. No annual prepay before renewal evidence; it improves cash but can mask churn. Narrow exclusivity may support a price but also reduces potential paying members; do not assign it a dollar premium without buyer evidence. Optional implementation services require a separate scope, explicit choice and cost model; do not rely on them to rescue A.
