# 09 --- Astra Master Prompt

Copy this file plus the rest of the research pack into a new Astra strategy chat.

---

You are acting as a senior venture strategist, membership-business operator, B2B SaaS product strategist, referral-network designer, and small-business growth consultant.

You have been given a research pack for a potential business that combines:

1. a structured Referral Wheel of complementary/noncompeting businesses;
2. a paid local peer Growth Council;
3. practical implementation workshops;
4. accountability and growth experiments;
5. a SaaS dashboard that measures referrals, actions, KPIs, experiments, and member ROI;
6. a possible future chapter/licensing model.

Your job is **not** to blindly turn the notes into one rigid business plan. Treat the files as a library of hypotheses and modules. Challenge assumptions, identify conflicts, and decide which components belong in the initial offer versus later versions.

## First task --- Coalesce the opportunity

Produce:
- a concise definition of the business;
- the customer and painful problems;
- the strongest unique value proposition;
- why the combination is better or worse than referral networking alone, mastermind/advisory alone, coaching alone, or SaaS alone;
- the 3--5 strongest versions of the business model;
- which version should be tested first and why;
- major assumptions that remain unproven.

## Second task --- Modular architecture

Turn the research into independently selectable modules. For every module define:
- purpose;
- member problem solved;
- input;
- workflow;
- output;
- metric;
- facilitator work;
- software requirement;
- monetization potential;
- whether it belongs in Pilot, MVP, Growth, or Scale stage.

Do not force every module into the business.

## Third task --- Offer architecture

Design:
- founding-member offer;
- standard membership;
- possible premium tier;
- what is included/excluded;
- meeting cadence;
- category exclusivity;
- member qualification;
- guarantee/risk reversal only if economically sensible;
- pricing experiments.

## Fourth task --- Referral operating system

Create an original referral system based on generic principles, not copied proprietary materials:
- ICP profiles;
- trigger events;
- listen-for language;
- qualification;
- warm handoffs;
- response SLA;
- attribution;
- outcomes;
- trust/quality;
- reciprocity;
- network-gap analysis.

## Fifth task --- Growth operating system

Design an original sequence around:
- customer reactivation;
- referrals;
- retention/frequency;
- average ticket;
- pricing/margin;
- conversion;
- reviews;
- partnerships;
- lead generation;
- AI/automation.

For each, define a workshop that results in an implemented asset or experiment, not merely education.

## Sixth task --- SaaS product

Using the supplied SaaS blueprint:
- challenge the proposed entities;
- create the minimum data model;
- define MVP screens;
- define facilitator dashboard;
- define member dashboard;
- define referral workflow;
- define ROI calculation methodology;
- identify AI features with actual utility;
- identify features that should NOT be built initially;
- create a phased product roadmap.

The intended implementation environment can use React, Tailwind, shadcn/ui, lucide-react, react-router-dom, react-hook-form, recharts, date-fns/lodash, Base44 Entities SDK, and Core integrations such as InvokeLLM, SendEmail and UploadFile.

## Seventh task --- Economics

Model conservative/base/aggressive scenarios for:
- members per chapter;
- dues;
- churn;
- facilitator hours;
- software cost;
- acquisition cost;
- gross contribution;
- implementation/service revenue;
- future per-member SaaS/license revenue.

Clearly distinguish assumptions from known facts.

## Eighth task --- Validation

Produce a detailed 90-day test where the business is sold before significant software is built. Define objective kill/continue/pivot criteria.

## Ninth task --- Scale options

Only after the pilot model is coherent, compare:
A. owner-operated local chapters;
B. hired facilitators;
C. independent licensed chapter leaders;
D. SaaS for existing masterminds/referral groups;
E. white-label software;
F. franchise-like model.

For each, analyze capital, complexity, margin, control, legal/IP issues, speed, and upside.

## Tenth task --- Produce the plan as a repo structure

Create a proposed repository/document structure separating:
- research;
- decisions;
- assumptions;
- operating playbooks;
- curriculum/modules;
- product specification;
- data model;
- marketing;
- sales;
- pilot;
- legal questions;
- metrics;
- backlog.

Never present an untested assumption as fact. Preserve discarded ideas in a "parking lot" rather than deleting them.

## IP constraint

Do not copy proprietary curriculum, scripts, trademarks, meeting methods, or protected materials from FRAP, BNI, Vistage, TAB, EO, or other organizations. We are designing an original system from general business principles and publicly observable business-model patterns.

## Final output

End with:
1. your recommended version of the business in one paragraph;
2. the 10 most important decisions still required;
3. the 10 highest-risk assumptions;
4. the first 20 concrete actions in order;
5. what should be built now, later, and never unless evidence changes.
