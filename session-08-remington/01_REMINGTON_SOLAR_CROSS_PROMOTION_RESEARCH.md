# Remington Solar Contractor Cross-Promotion Opportunity

> **Project status:** Research-only business option completed September 15, 2026. The candidate rankings are provisional analytical judgments, not founder selections or evidence that any company will participate. No outreach, list access, campaign, partner recruitment, software build, or real-world pilot is authorized. The validation design below is a hypothetical framework for later evaluation only.

## Executive finding

The opportunity is real, but the strongest version is not a lead-list exchange. It is a **permissioned business-to-business channel partnership** in which each participating company markets a complementary contractor offer to its own audience, retains custody of its contacts, and sends interested contractors to a tracked, co-branded landing page.

Remington Solar is unusually suitable as the first test case. Its public website confirms a dealer program for roofers, insulation contractors, HVAC companies, solar installers, general contractors, retailers, and distributors. Approved dealers can receive a pitch-board sales tool with a miniature demonstration fan and homeowner leads generated by Remington's national retail sales. Remington also operates a public affiliate program paying 5% on referred product sales.[^1][^2] The Facebook offer of a *free* demonstration kit was observed directly but could not be independently verified on Remington's public site; the public dealer page confirms the demo tool but does not state that it is free.

The best prospective counterparts are not generic lead sellers. They are:

1. contractor software platforms that publicly recruit manufacturing or strategic partners;
2. complementary product manufacturers with their own contractor/dealer programs;
3. roofing and solar media networks that can run a controlled paid validation campaign; and
4. installer-training or dealer networks serving the same trades.

The best first conversations, in order, are **Roofr, JobNimbus, RoofTalk, RainDrop Gutter Guard, and BirdBlocker**. RoofersCoffeeShop is the best paid validation channel if a reciprocal partner is not immediately available.

The concept should proceed only after two gates are satisfied:

- an advertising review substantiates the product's energy, moisture, temperature, and savings claims; and
- counsel approves the compensation and consent design for the states included in the pilot.

## What Remington Solar has already proven

Remington's public materials validate several components of the proposed model:

- It is actively building a network of roofers, contractors, and home-improvement professionals.[^1]
- Its contractor funnel verifies the applicant's business type, general-liability coverage, anticipated fan volume, and agreement to minimum advertised pricing.[^2]
- Approved dealers are offered wholesale pricing, a pitch-board sales tool with a miniature demonstration fan, warranty handling, and homeowner leads routed by territory.[^2]
- It invites internet marketers into a separate affiliate program paying 5% on referred product sales.[^3]

These are important because they show that Remington already understands channel acquisition, dealer enablement, lead routing, and performance compensation. The proposed business would not need to convince Remington to invent affiliate or dealer marketing from scratch. It would offer a new source of qualified dealer applicants and a reciprocal-promotion framework.

What is **not** established publicly is equally important:

- The size, engagement, or geographic distribution of Remington's contractor list is not disclosed.
- No public evidence confirms that Remington will promote third-party products to its dealers.
- The demo pitch board is confirmed; a currently free kit is not confirmed outside the observed Facebook advertisement.
- Public evidence does not establish dealer conversion, activation, reorder, installation, or homeowner-lead close rates.

Therefore, Remington should be treated as a promising launch partner—not as a proven distribution engine until those figures are disclosed under an appropriate agreement.

## Recommended business model

### Contractor Revenue Exchange

The business acts as a curator and campaign operator connecting noncompeting vendors that sell through the same contractor channel. It does not sell roofing, solar, or home-improvement work; choose contractors for homeowners; negotiate consumer contracts; or take a share of a homeowner's project price.

Each campaign would work as follows:

1. Vendor A approves Vendor B as a complementary offer.
2. Vendor A sends the promotion to its own opted-in contractors from Vendor A's own systems.
3. Contractors click to a dedicated landing page that identifies both companies and clearly states who will contact them.
4. A contractor affirmatively requests information, a demo, pricing, or a dealer application.
5. Only the information submitted for that request is transmitted to the named recipient.
6. Campaign reporting uses aggregate delivery data plus attributed opt-ins and B2B conversions.

This model avoids the most problematic premise in the original idea: that an audience owner's list can simply be given or sold to another vendor. Companies with valuable lists are unlikely to do that, their privacy promises may prohibit it, and transferring personal contact information creates unnecessary compliance and trust problems.

### Revenue options, from cleanest to most sensitive

| Model | Description | Relative risk | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Fixed campaign-management fee | A vendor pays for partner identification, campaign design, tracking, reporting, and administration. | Low | Best starting revenue |
| Fixed sponsorship fee | The promoted vendor pays the audience owner for a newsletter placement, webinar, dealer-resource listing, or kit insert. | Low–moderate | Good after audience and deliverables are documented |
| Reciprocal in-kind placement | Each vendor promotes the other once to an agreed segment. | Low | Best proof-of-concept when neither party wants a cash commitment |
| Fixed B2B activation bounty | Payment occurs when a contractor independently opens an approved dealer account or completes a first wholesale purchase. | Moderate | Potentially attractive; require state-specific legal review |
| Affiliate percentage on product sale | Compensation follows a direct product purchase through a tracked link; Remington publicly offers 5%. | Moderate | Usable for direct product commerce, with disclosure and contract review |
| Homeowner appointment or completed-project fee | Payment depends on a consumer home-improvement lead, appointment, contract, or payment. | High | Do not include in the initial model |
| Percentage of a contractor's homeowner job | Compensation is tied to the home-improvement contract value. | Highest | Exclude |

The cleanest launch therefore combines a fixed project fee with either reciprocal promotion or a fixed B2B dealer-activation amount. If the 5% affiliate program is used, the campaign should clearly disclose the financial relationship and keep the tracked transaction limited to product commerce.

### Why Roofr is the strongest first target

Roofr is the clearest strategic match because its partner language describes the exact role Remington needs: manufacturing partners that help roofing contractors increase demand and sales of their products. Roofr also operates estimating, proposals, CRM, payments, and material-ordering workflows, so the partnership can become more useful than a one-time email.[^14]

A practical pilot does not require software engineering. It could begin with a Remington line-item template, product education, a sample proposal page, and a tracked dealer-kit offer. If engagement is meaningful, the parties could later explore a marketplace or workflow integration.

### Why JobNimbus is nearly as strong

JobNimbus has both scale and explicit partnership flexibility. Its four public partner tracks permit a strategic or service/marketing test before anyone discusses an integration. Its published 2026 materials say more than 8,000 contractors use the platform and name roofing, gutters, solar, and other relevant trades.[^4]

The strongest pitch is not “give us your list.” It is “help your contractors add a warrantied, proposal-ready revenue item; we will supply the education, sales asset, fulfillment, tracking, and support.” That aligns with JobNimbus's public stated preference for partners that serve contractors and fit real workflows.

### Why product partners matter

RainDrop, Gutterglove, BirdBlocker, VELUX, and Gemstone offer the best reciprocity. Each needs more contractors to carry or install its product. Each has a distinct consumer benefit, and none of the selected product lines duplicates a solar attic fan.

The reciprocal audience logic is strongest when contractor segments are matched carefully:

## Candidates to exclude or defer

### Direct competitors

Solatube should not be treated as a noncompetitive skylight partner. Although it has a strong dealer and preferred-contractor program, it also sells solar attic fans and other ventilation products.[^15] The same exclusion should apply to solar-attic-fan and powered-attic-ventilation manufacturers such as Attic Breeze, QuietCool's relevant ventilation lines, and Solar Royal.

### Generic homeowner lead sellers

Angi/HomeAdvisor, Modernize, Networx, and similar marketplaces may have contractor and homeowner volume, but their economic model, lead permissions, exclusivity terms, and state contractor-referral rules make them poor first pilots. They also introduce the very ambiguity this concept can avoid: whether compensation is tied to a homeowner's service appointment or completed home-improvement contract.

### Large distributors and roofing manufacturers

ABC Supply, Beacon/QXO, SRS Distribution, Owens Corning, GAF, and CertainTeed have enormous channel reach. They should be deferred until a small pilot produces real activation and reorder data. Their procurement cycles are longer, they have existing vendor relationships, and they are less likely to test a loosely defined reciprocal promotion.

### Contractor Nation umbrella

RainDrop's parent-network relationship is useful evidence of partnership culture, but the larger Contractor Nation organization includes Attic Systems and Dr. Energy Saver. Those offerings overlap with attic insulation and home-energy retrofits and may create conflict with Remington's broader product line.[^16] Approach RainDrop first rather than pitching the umbrella network.

## The clean campaign architecture

### Data custody

- Each audience owner sends from its own system.
- No CSV, list rental, enrichment file, or raw audience export is transferred.
- The landing page names the promoted vendor before any form submission.
- The form states exactly which company or companies may contact the contractor and through which channels.
- Marketing consent is unbundled from the request for a demo or pricing wherever required.
- Suppression lists and opt-outs remain enforceable across the campaign.

This architecture is commercially sensible even before legal analysis: it protects the asset that makes the partnership valuable, lets the audience owner preserve trust, and gives the promoted vendor only self-identified prospects.

### Attribution

Use a unique campaign URL and store only the minimum attribution fields:

- source partner;
- campaign and creative version;
- contractor-declared trade and state;
- demo/pricing/application request;
- dealer approval;
- first wholesale order; and
- 60- or 90-day reorder status.

Do not measure success only by raw leads. The key outcome is **activated contractor accounts that reorder**. A free demo request can be cheap and low-intent; a dealer that buys, installs correctly, and reorders is the economic unit.

## Hypothetical validation design (not authorized)

### Phase 1: confirm the offer

Before recruiting a counterpart, obtain a written Remington partner sheet covering:

- which contractor trades and states are accepted;
- whether the demo pitch board is free, subsidized, refundable, or purchase-required;
- retail price, dealer price, expected gross profit, installation time, and common labor charge;
- current dealer count and contractor-list size by trade and state;
- kit-request, dealer-approval, first-order, installation, and reorder conversion rates;
- homeowner-lead routing rules;
- warranty and claim-handling responsibilities;
- advertising claim substantiation; and
- whether Remington will reciprocally promote an approved noncompetitive partner.

### Phase 2: one audience, one offer, one state group

Run a deliberately small campaign with either Roofr/JobNimbus or a media channel:

- one educational landing page;
- one contractor email or newsletter placement;
- one follow-up only to contractors who affirmatively opted in;
- one demo/pricing CTA;
- 30-day activation reporting; and
- 90-day reorder reporting.

The go/no-go metrics should be agreed before launch. Suggested internal thresholds—not industry benchmarks—are:

| Metric | Initial decision threshold |
| --- | ---: |
| Qualified landing-page visitors | 100+ |
| Demo/pricing requests | 15%+ of qualified visitors |
| Verified dealer applications | 40%+ of requests |
| Approved dealer first orders | 30%+ of applications |
| 90-day reorder or documented installation | 40%+ of first orders |
| Complaints / partner-trust incidents | 0 material incidents |

These thresholds are intentionally demanding. The purpose of the first campaign is not to produce impressive click volume; it is to show that the offer creates durable B2B customers without harming the partner's audience.

### Phase 3: product-to-product reciprocity

If the first test works, pair Remington with one complementary product vendor, ideally RainDrop, Gutterglove Pro, or BirdBlocker. Each party sends one promotion to a matched trade segment while keeping its own list. The campaign operator manages creative consistency, attribution, opt-out requirements, reporting, and the reciprocal-placement ledger.

## Legal and compliance boundaries

This is a business-model risk screen, not legal advice. State contractor, referral, telemarketing, privacy, advertising, and licensing rules must be reviewed for each campaign.

### Commercial email

The CAN-SPAM Act applies to commercial email, including business-to-business messages. The FTC requires accurate header and subject information, identification of the message as advertising, a valid postal address, a working opt-out mechanism, and prompt honoring of opt-outs. The FTC also warns that both the promoted company and the company sending on its behalf can be responsible.[^17]

That supports the recommended model: a written agreement should designate the sender, assign suppression-list responsibilities, approve creative, and give both parties audit rights. No party should assume it can contract away compliance.

### Calls and texts

Marketing calls and texts introduce a higher-risk consent layer. The FCC states that prior written consent is required for covered robocalls and robotexts.[^18] The first pilot should therefore be email- and web-form-led. Telephone or SMS follow-up should occur only when the contractor's form clearly authorizes the named company and channel, and counsel has approved the workflow.

### Privacy and list sharing

Covered businesses under California privacy law must honor applicable rights concerning the sale or sharing of personal information.[^19] Other states have their own privacy laws. Avoiding a raw list transfer does not eliminate every obligation, but it substantially reduces the number of parties collecting and repurposing personal contact information.

### Affiliate and sponsorship disclosure

The FTC treats financial or other material relationships as facts that may need clear disclosure when they could affect the credibility of a recommendation.[^20] A paid newsletter, affiliate link, free product, or reciprocal promotional benefit should be disclosed plainly near the recommendation.

### Contractor-referral compensation

Do not infer that a B2B structure automatically resolves every contractor-law issue. The initial agreements should expressly separate compensation from any homeowner's contract, project price, appointment, payment, or contractor selection. A fixed fee for campaign services or for a contractor's B2B dealer activation is conceptually cleaner, but California and other state counsel should confirm the actual facts, especially if Remington routes homeowner leads or if a campaign later includes consumer referrals.

## Product-claim diligence gate

The most important non-obvious risk is the product narrative itself. Remington publicly claims that its fans can reduce attic temperature, reduce cooling costs, reduce HVAC strain, and prevent moisture damage.[^1] Those claims require substantiation appropriate to the exact product, climate, attic construction, intake ventilation, ceiling air sealing, and installation conditions.

The U.S. Department of Energy's Building America Solution Center says powered attic ventilators can lower attic temperature, but the incremental cooling benefit is usually small and can be outweighed by negative-pressure effects. It states that attic fans often cause more overall energy loss than they save, can increase winter moisture problems, and may pull conditioned air or combustion gases when the building enclosure and intake ventilation are inadequate.[^21] ENERGY STAR similarly warns that blocked soffit vents or poor air sealing can cause an attic fan to pull conditioned air out of the house and increase air-conditioning use.[^22]

This does not prove that Remington's product is ineffective in every properly selected installation. It does mean that a national partner should not repeat universal energy-savings or moisture-prevention promises without documentation. Before scaling, require:

- product-specific independent test reports;
- precise installation prerequisites;
- climate and attic-type limitations;
- soffit/intake requirements;
- combustion-safety guidance;
- warranty exclusions and callback history; and
- approved claims and disclaimers for contractor presentations.

A more defensible sales message may be “an active ventilation option for suitable, properly air-sealed and adequately supplied attics” rather than a universal utility-bill savings promise.

## Bottom line

This is a better national business concept than selling homeowner referrals because the valuable transaction can occur entirely in the B2B channel: one vendor helps another acquire and activate contractors. The operating asset is not a secret list. It is a trusted network of audience owners, a screening process for complementary offers, a compliant campaign system, and reliable activation data.

If this option is later selected and real-world validation is separately authorized, **Roofr** is the strongest first candidate because its manufacturing-partner language most directly matches the proposed use case. **JobNimbus** ranks second for scale and multiple partnership tracks. **RoofTalk** or **RoofersCoffeeShop** could support a controlled market test. **RainDrop/Gutterglove** or **BirdBlocker** are the leading reciprocal-product hypotheses.

Do not buy lists, request raw list access, or begin with homeowner appointments. Do not scale until Remington supplies product-claim substantiation and real dealer-funnel data. If those gates are passed, the concept can expand beyond one manufacturer into a national “contractor revenue exchange” for vetted, noncompeting add-on products.
