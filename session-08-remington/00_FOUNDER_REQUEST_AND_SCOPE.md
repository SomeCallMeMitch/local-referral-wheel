# Session 8 founder request and scope

Recorded September 15, 2026.

## Founder observation

The founder found a Remington Solar Facebook advertisement offering roofing and solar contractors a free demonstration kit for solar attic fans. The advertisement positioned the product as an add-on contractors could include in homeowner presentations to increase sales.

The founder identified a possible national variation of the referral concept: find companies that already market noncompeting products or services to the same contractor audience, then arrange cross-promotion between their permissioned audiences. The founder asked which companies have comparable contractor offers and public evidence that they may be open to partnership.

## Research assignment

- Verify Remington Solar's public dealer, demonstration-tool, lead-routing, and affiliate offers.
- Identify noncompetitive companies serving roofers, solar installers, or adjacent exterior contractors.
- Distinguish public partnership evidence from speculation about willingness.
- Design a privacy-conscious cross-promotion structure that does not require selling or transferring contact lists.
- Compare possible operator revenue mechanisms without assuming they are lawful or commercially accepted.
- Identify legal, advertising, consent, and product-claim risks.

## Governing project limits

This session is public-source evaluation only. It does not authorize contacting Remington Solar or any candidate, applying to partner programs, submitting forms, requesting kits, accessing or transferring lists, launching campaigns, recruiting contractors, creating consumer referrals, or conducting real-world tests.

All candidate rankings are provisional research judgments. No company has agreed to participate, no partner has been selected, and no revenue mechanism has received legal clearance. Any future pilot described in the research is hypothetical unless the founder separately changes the project's governing scope.

## Relationship to the existing project

This is an adjacent national B2B channel model, not a replacement for the local Benicia referral configurations. It may reduce some risks by keeping the principal transaction between vendors and contractors, but it does not erase California contractor-referral rules when compensation or activity becomes connected to homeowner leads, appointments, contracts, project value, or contractor selection.

The strongest structural distinction is data custody: each audience owner would send its own promotion, and only contractors who affirmatively request information would submit data to the named recipient. The proposed asset is a vetted partner network and campaign operating system—not possession of third-party contractor lists.
