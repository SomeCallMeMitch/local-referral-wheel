## Score rationale and sensitivity

- HLWF earns clean-boundary and owner points; individually readable recent reviews and demonstrated post-sale correction are its weaknesses.
- LLO has the strongest organizing audit, but its existing vendor relationships, optional ancillary work and unverified city-level delivery reduce configuration confidence.
- HMH has relatively recent original-platform detail and multiple team members, but broad scope and inaccessible low reviews prevent a high-confidence endorsement.
- BFC wins the provisional flooring position through narrow category fit, not review-audit superiority. A current adverse pattern or unavailable service would remove it.
- Solano's historic recovery example merits care points; a 2025 pair of reviews does not make the whole reputation recent.
- Simplified Spaces and Hobson remain plausible local alternatives with insufficient review coverage.
- Floors on First's old complaints are material questions, not assertions of current behavior.
- Capacity scores are deliberately low across the table: active websites, forms or small teams do not prove available work slots or willingness.
- No candidate earns high transferability. A named owner relationship is an access advantage and a key-person risk.

If specific Benicia service is mandatory before even a provisional shortlist, omit LLO from the eligible list and use the weaker local-only organizing configuration. If a complete low-review and license audit is mandatory before a named recommendation, **none is cleared**.

## Evidence quality, separate from fit score and confidence

Quality grade assesses the material itself: A = recent, detailed, broad original-platform coverage with negative population and identity continuity checked; B = substantial original detail with material coverage/recency gaps; C = thin, old, selected or derivative evidence; D = discovery-only. No candidate earns A. Confidence describes how firmly the current recommendation follows from that incomplete material, not a statistical probability.
