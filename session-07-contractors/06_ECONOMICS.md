## Required collected wins

Invented **$250, $750 and $1,250 collected compensation points** are sensitivity thresholds only. They are not quotes, available fees, lawful contractor commissions, percentages, or owner commitments.

Required wins = ceiling(monthly gross-receipts target / collected compensation per win).

| Monthly founder gross-receipts target | At $250 | At $750 | At $1,250 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $4,000 | 16 | 6 | 4 |
| $7,500 | 30 | 10 | 6 |
| $10,000 | 40 | 14 | 8 |

At $750, the annual equivalents of sustaining those rounded monthly wins are 72, 120 and 168 collected wins. At $1,250 they are 48, 72 and 96. Cancellations, refunds, taxes, founder expenses and collection delays increase the burden. Closed is not completed, and completed is not collected.

### Qualification and collection sensitivity

These conversion rates are invented, not local evidence. A qualifying introduction means permission, real fit, a plausible budget and timeframe, and no known duplication—not simply a name.

| Target at $750 | Collected wins/month | If 25% of qualifying introductions become collected wins | If 10% do |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $4,000 | 6 | 24 introductions/month | 60 |
| $7,500 | 10 | 40 | 100 |
| $10,000 | 14 | 56 | 140 |

A transparent illustrative chain is 60 distinct households/month across the partners × 25% suitable expressed adjacent need × 80% permission × 40% recipient wins × 90% completes/collects = **4.32 expected collected wins**, or $3,240 at the hypothetical $750 point. Every input is invented; this is **not a forecast**. The same family seen by three providers must not count as three unique opportunities.

Doubling household encounters does not guarantee double income: existing referrals, capacity, budget conflicts and geographic fit reduce incremental volume. Public review counts cannot estimate household throughput or conversion.

### Contribution test, not a margin assumption

For any future permissible acquisition arrangement, available economic surplus would have to cover actual fulfillment costs, sales time, expected rework/warranty costs, overhead allocation and required operating profit **before** deciding an affordable acquisition spend. No public source gives those inputs for these businesses.

For illustration only, $750 is 15% of a $5,000 job's revenue or 5% of a $15,000 job's revenue. These ratios are not profit margins and not proposed commissions. A profitable company may still have no incremental capacity or reason to buy this source.

## Timing, founder workload and concentration

Custom products and transitions can create months between first discussion and collection. A weekly call system cannot assume same-month cash receipts or make clients buy sooner. Four providers do not diversify risk if one organizer supplies most introductions or one specialist produces most collectible outcomes.

Illustrative workload at four 15-minute partner conversations plus 30 minutes of status administration is 1.5 hours/week before exceptions. This is not validated. One disputed installation, move deadline or missing payment can exceed the entire planned weekly allowance. Keeping technical support with providers is essential, but reputational spillover still reaches the founder.

The dated [Handymen suspension notice](https://handymen.repair/) is a concrete counterweight to assuming small local firms simply need more leads. It does not establish other candidates' finances. The [Zillow team activity display](https://www.zillow.com/profile/Shandrikapowell) is also not evidence of a large monthly source stream; its eight last-year team sales are a platform snapshot, not a complete census.

**Economic recommendation:** preserve the client-care pair as a relationship hypothesis; do not underwrite $4,000, $7,500 or $10,000 monthly compensation from this public evidence. Do not invent monthly dues, subscriptions or a fee label to rescue an unproven model or bypass the separate legal analysis.
